## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. Decompose a 3-digit number in more than one way (by place value and other ways), with parts that add back to the number.
2. Subtract by decomposing the number being taken away and subtracting its parts one at a time, writing each step.
3. Choose a helpful decomposition — by place value, or a first part that lands on a hundred or ten ("friendly number").
4. Know the difference is the same however the number is decomposed and in whatever order the parts are taken away.
5. Find decomposition mistakes (parts that don't add back to the number).
6. Solve subtraction story problems ("the rest", "difference between greatest and least" from a table).

Prerequisites: 2-6 (decompose by place value), subtracting multiples of 10 and 100, basic subtraction facts.

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Decompose** — break a number into parts. 175 = 100 + 70 + 5.
- **Part** — one of the pieces you break a number into. The parts must add back to the number.
- **Difference** — the answer when you subtract.
- **Subtract** — take away.
- **Friendly number** — a number that is easy to work with, like 200 or 190.
- **Decomposition tree** — a picture with the number on top and arrows down to its parts.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

1. **Decomposition tree:** one number on top, its parts below. Different trees for the same number (Be Curious: same hexagon, different blocks). Book: 184 → 100, 80, 4 and 184 → 153, 30, 1.
2. **Subtract the parts by place value (One Way):** take away the hundreds, then the tens, then the ones, writing each step. Book: 353 − 100 = 253, − 80 = 173, − 4 = 169.
3. **Subtract parts that make friendly numbers (Another Way):** choose a first part that matches the end of the starting number so you land on a hundred (353 − 153 = 200), then take the rest (− 30 = 170, − 1 = 169).
4. **Same difference:** all the parts together are still the whole number taken away, so the answer does not change (Math is… Explaining); the order of the parts does not matter either (item 12).
5. **Check your parts:** the parts must add back to the number you are taking away (item 9: 100 + 10 = 110, not 101).

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Five steps. Demo numbers (Your Turn avoids): 175, 362 − 175, 458 − 102, 538 − 215, 276 − 139, and the book's 184, 353 − 184, 572 − 378, 345 − 101, 438 − 342.

Your Turn generator ("DS pair"): a in 300–999, b in 101–(a − 100), a − b ≥ 100, b has non-zero tens and ones, and at least one place needs regrouping (ones of b > ones of a, or tens of b > tens of a), (a, b) not a demo pair.

### Step 1 — Break a number apart in different ways
Example parts:
1. Be Curious idea: two hexagons built from different blocks — "Same shape, different parts." (simple SVG/CSS, optional; or skip and just say it)
2. Tree (model M-DTREE, §13): **175 → 100, 70, 5**. "By place value."
3. Tree: **175 → 162, 10, 3**. "Another way. Check: 162 + 10 + 3 = 175."
4. Tree: **175 → 150, 25**. "Two parts is fine too. The parts must always add back to 175."
Your Turn: M-DTREE in **tree-only mode**, two trees for one random 3-digit n (non-zero tens and ones): "Break n apart in 2 different ways." Grading: each tree has 2–4 non-zero whole-number parts (blank or 0 boxes are ignored, §13) that add to n, and the two trees are not the same set of parts (order ignored). Hint: "Try place value first. Then try a different way." Explanation shows the place-value way (n = H + T + O) and one other example: (n − 10) + 10.

### Step 2 — Take away the parts, one at a time (place value)
Example parts:
1. "362 − 175. Break apart 175: 100, 70, 5." (tree)
2. "362 − 100 = **262**."
3. "262 − 70 = **192**."
4. "192 − 5 = **187**. So 362 − 175 = 187."
Your Turn: M-DTREE **subtract mode** with the tree **pre-filled by place value** for a DS pair; the child types each step result and the difference. Grading: each step result = previous − part; final = a − b. Hint: "Take away the hundreds first, then the tens, then the ones."

### Step 3 — Land on a friendly number
Example parts:
1. "362 − 175 again. This time break 175 into **162, 10, 3**. Why 162? Because 362 − 162 is easy!"
2. "362 − 162 = **200**. 200 − 10 = **190**. 190 − 3 = **187**."
3. "Same difference: **187**. All the parts together still make 175."
Your Turn: M-DTREE subtract mode, child makes their **own** tree for a DS pair, then the steps. Grading (any decomposition): see §13 — 2–4 non-zero parts that add to b (blank/0 boxes ignored), every step consistent, final = a − b. Then `choice` "Why is the answer the same with different parts?" → "All the parts together still make the same number, so I take away the same amount." (wrong: "Because the hundreds part is always the same." · "It isn't always the same." · "Because I took away the ones first.") Hint: "Can you pick a first part that makes a hundred?"

### Step 4 — Any order, and check your parts
Example parts:
1. "538 − 215. Take away 5, then 200, then 10: 533, 333, **323**. Take away 200, 10, 5: 338, 328, **323**. Any order works, because you still take away 215 in all."
2. "Eli subtracts **458 − 102**. He breaks 102 into **100 and 20**. Check: 100 + 20 = 120, not 102! He should use **100 and 2**: 458 − 100 = 358, 358 − 2 = **356**."
Your Turn: `parts` generated: start a random whole number 200–999 (so a − b ≥ 91), not a demo number; b = 100 + d (d 1–9, so b is 101–109); a student "breaks b into 100 and d×10". Parts: `choice` "What is the mistake?" → "100 + (10×d) = [100 + 10d], not [b]. The parts must add to [b]." (rendered with real numbers, e.g. d = 4: "100 + 40 = 140, not 104. The parts must add to 104.") wrong choices (real numbers): "You can't break apart [b]." / "He should take away the 100 last." / "Nothing — the parts are right." ; `num` "The correct difference" → a − b. Plus `choice` "Can you take away the parts in any order?" → "Yes. You still take away the whole number." (wrong: "No. Always take away the hundreds first." · "No. The order changes the answer.") Hint: "Add the parts. Do they make the number?"

### Step 5 — Story problems
Example parts:
1. Table "Book Fair Visitors": Mon 126, Tue 184, Wed 239, Thu 207. "Greatest: 239. Least: 126. 239 − 126: break 126 into 100, 20, 6 → 139, 119, **113**."
2. "276 marbles. 139 are green. The rest are red. 276 − 139: break 139 into 76, 60, 3 → 200, 140, **137** red marbles." (76 + 60 + 3 = 139)
Your Turn: generated table of 4 days with 3-digit counts (all different, max − min between 40 and 300); `parts`: `num` "Greatest", `num` "Least", `num` "Difference". Hint: "Find the biggest and the smallest first."

Learn reflection: "How do you decide how to break apart the number?" Idea: "Place value always works. If a part can make a hundred or a ten, that can be quicker."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Parts that don't add back to the number (100 + 10 for 101). → "Add your parts. Do they make the number?"
- Subtracting a part from the original number every time instead of from the last result (362 − 100, then 362 − 70). → Each step starts with the last answer.
- Decomposing both numbers and subtracting place by place (this is not this lesson's strategy and fails with regrouping: 362 − 175 → 200, 10, −3). → Keep the first number whole; break apart only the number you take away.
- Thinking the order changes the answer. → Show two orders give the same result.
- In tables, subtracting the first and last rows instead of greatest and least.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal:** Subtract 3-digit numbers by breaking apart the number being taken away and taking away the parts one at a time.
- **Words:** decompose, part, difference, friendly number, decomposition tree.
- **Demonstrate:** (book example) 353 − 184. Tree 184 → 100, 80, 4: 253, 173, 169. Then tree 184 → 153, 30, 1: 200, 170, 169. Same answer. Then show the order doesn't matter (take 4 first). Then show a broken tree (101 → 100, 10) and ask what's wrong.
- **Ask:** "Do your parts add back to the number?" · "What's the easiest part to take away first?" · "Can you land on a hundred?" · "Why is the answer the same both ways?"
- **Checklist:** decomposes a number 2 ways · subtracts by place-value parts · uses a friendly-number part · explains same difference / any order · spots parts that don't add up · solves table and "the rest" problems.

## 13. New interaction or model needed

**M-DTREE — decomposition tree + subtraction chain (new question type `dsub`; tree-only mode shared with any "decompose in 2 ways" item):**
- **Tree:** top box shows the number n; below, 2–4 input boxes (start with 3, "+ add a part" / "remove" buttons, min 2, max 4) joined by arrows. Live check line under the tree: "Your parts add to ___" (shows the running total; no right/wrong colour until Check).
- **Blank and 0 parts:** before grading, ignore any part box that is blank or 0. A tree is valid when **at least 2 non-zero parts remain** (at most 4), each a whole number ≥ 1, and they sum to n. So 610 entered as 600 / 10 / 0 is graded as 600 / 10 (correct); the 0 box gets a gentle note "You can remove the 0 part" that is not counted as a miss. A tree with fewer than 2 non-zero parts (e.g. 610 / 0 / blank) is "not broken apart yet" (a miss). In subtract mode the step row for an ignored part is not shown (or shown greyed and not graded). **Unit tests:** 953 − 610 with tree 600 / 10 / 0 and steps 353, 343 → correct; 728 − 460 with 400 / 60 / 0 and steps 328, 268 → correct.
- **Tree-only mode** (`trees: 2`): two trees side by side. Correct when each tree is valid (as above) and the two trees are not the same multiset of non-zero parts.
- **Subtract mode** (`a`, `b`): tree for b (pre-filled with `given: [..]` or blank for the child's own), then one step row per part, in the tree's order: `[start] − [part] = [ ]`, where `start` is a for the first row and the previous row's result (as typed) afterwards; the part is copied in automatically from the tree. Final line `a − b = [ ]`.
  - Correct when: parts valid for b (as above); each typed result = (previous typed result) − part, and ≥ 0; final = a − b. A miss gives a specific message: "Your parts add to 110, not 101" / "Check 262 − 70" / "Your last step shows 187 — write it as the answer."
  - `twoWays: true` → two subtract blocks; both must be correct and the trees must differ.
- `correctText`: place-value decomposition with its steps, plus "(any parts that add to b work)".
- Visual-only variant for Learn slides (tree with fixed parts; steps revealed one per slide part).

## 14. Open questions / assumptions

- The lesson teaches decomposing the number being **subtracted** (as the book does). Decomposing the first number is not offered.
- Allowing 2–4 non-zero parts; blank or 0 boxes are ignored (coordinator ruling on B-01). A single non-zero part (just "175") is not a decomposition.
- Steps are graded against the child's own previous result so one slip is reported once, not carried; the final answer still must be exactly a − b. Builder should confirm this matches how 2-2's staged items report errors.
- "Why did you choose that way?" (book 3–4) is a structured choice with one true reason; the child's real reason may differ — parent can talk about it in Practice Together.
