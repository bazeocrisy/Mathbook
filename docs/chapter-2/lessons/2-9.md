## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. Show a subtraction story with a bar diagram: the whole (longer bar) and a known part; the unknown part is the dashed "?" piece.
2. Write a subtraction equation with an unknown difference **and** the related addition equation with an unknown addend for the same diagram.
3. Find the unknown addend by **counting up** (add on in friendly jumps), including across zeros (`400 − 153`).
4. Use the relationship when the unknown is in a different place (`452 − ? = 237` → `237 + ? = 452`) and when the equation is written backward (`___ = 300 − 179`).
5. Spot the common error of adding the two given numbers.
6. Solve "how many more" (compare) and "how many left" (part-whole) word problems.

Prerequisites: 3-digit place value; adding within 1,000 (2-4 to 2-6); decompose/adjust to subtract (2-7, 2-8).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Bar diagram** — a drawing with bars that shows how the numbers in a story fit together.
- **Whole** — the total amount; the longest bar.
- **Part** — one piece of the whole.
- **Addend** — a number you add. In `248 + ? = 563`, 248 and ? are addends.
- **Unknown** — the number we don't know yet. We write it as **?** (or a letter later).
- **Difference** — the answer to a subtraction problem.
- **Related equations** — addition and subtraction equations that use the same three numbers (`248 + 315 = 563`, `563 − 248 = 315`).
- **Count up** — start at the smaller number and add jumps until you reach the bigger number.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

1. **Bar diagram for a take-from/part-whole story.** Book example: 575 miles in all, 246 driven. Whole bar 575 on top; part bar 246 below, left-aligned; dashed bracket from the end of the 246 bar to the end of the whole, labeled "?". The ? is the miles left.
2. **Subtraction with an unknown difference:** whole − known part = ? (`575 − 246 = ?`).
3. **Addition with an unknown addend:** known part + ? = whole (`246 + ? = 575`). Same diagram, same answer (329). Reasoning: the two parts together make the whole, so "what do I add to 246 to get 575?" is the same question as "575 take away 246".
4. **Solving the unknown addend** (book does not show the arithmetic; the app teaches counting up, consistent with 2-7/2-8): `246 + 4 = 250`, `250 + 50 = 300`, `300 + 275 = 575`; the jumps `4 + 50 + 275 = 329`. Any correct method is accepted; only the final answer is graded.
5. **Check**: `246 + 329 = 575`, so `575 − 246 = 329` is right.

(App Learn uses the same methods with original numbers: 563 − 248 = 315. The Parent Guide may cite "Book example: 575 − 246 = 329".)

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Six steps. Every Your Turn uses numbers not used in the Examples (Examples: 563, 248, 315, 400, 153, 247, 452, 237, 215, 541, 268, 273, 302, 458, 156). All Your Turn answers are graded on the final numbers only.

### Step 1 — Show a story with a bar diagram
Example slides:
1. Story card: "A class is reading a book with **563** pages. They have read **248** pages. How many pages are left?"
2. Bar diagram (compare layout, whole on top): top bar **563** (full width), lower bar **248** (≈44% width, left-aligned), dashed bracket to the end labeled **?**. Say: "The long bar is the whole book. The short bar is the part they read."
3. "The dashed part is the pages that are **left**. That's the unknown, **?**."

Your Turn (`parts`): a generated story "A ___ has W ___. P are ___. How many are left?" (contexts: pages in a book/read, seats in a theater/filled, stickers/used). Display: compare bar diagram with **blank slots A (top bar) and B (lower bar)** and "?" on the dashed piece.
- `num` "Box A (the whole):" = W; `num` "Box B (the part we know):" = P; `choice` "What does the ? stand for?" — correct "the part that is left" vs "the whole" / "the two numbers added together".
- Generation: W 400–900, P 120–(W−100), W, P not multiples of 100 (except W may be).
- Hint: "The whole is the biggest number. It goes in the long bar."

### Step 2 — Two equations, one diagram
Example slides:
1. Same diagram. "You can subtract: **563 − 248 = ?**" (subtraction with an unknown difference).
2. "Or you can add: **248 + ? = 563**" (addition with an unknown addend). Show both equations pointing at the same dashed piece.
3. "Both ask the same question: what goes with 248 to make 563?" The answer, revealed: **315** in both.

Your Turn (`parts`): "`W − P = ?`. Which addition equation is related?" `choice`: `P + ? = W` ✓; `W + P = ?`; `W + ? = P`; `? − P = W` (shuffled). Generation: W 300–999, P 100–W−50.
Hint: "Start with the part you know. What do you add to get the whole?"

### Step 3 — Count up to find the unknown addend
Example slides:
1. `248 + ? = 563`. "Count up from 248 in friendly jumps."
2. Jumps (optional number-line hops, see §13): `248 + 2 = 250` → `250 + 50 = 300` → `300 + 263 = 563`.
3. "Add the jumps: 2 + 50 + 263 = **315**."
4. Check: `248 + 315 = 563` ✓, so `563 − 248 = 315`.

Your Turn (`parts`): "Use addition to find `W − P`." Parts: `num` "P + ? = W, so ? =" (answer W−P). Generation: W 300–950, P 100–W−60, P mod 10 ≠ 0, W−P ≥ 50 and the ones digit of P greater than the ones digit of W at least half the time (regrouping case).
Hint: "Start at P. Jump to the next ten, then to the next hundred if you can, then to W. Add your jumps."

### Step 4 — Count up across zeros
Example slides:
1. `400 − 153 = ?` "Taking away from 400 is tricky. Count up instead: `153 + ? = 400`."
2. `153 + 7 = 160`, `160 + 40 = 200`, `200 + 200 = 400`. Jumps: `7 + 40 + 200 = 247`.
3. "The answer can sit on the left, too: `___ = 400 − 153` means the same thing. ___ = **247**."

Your Turn (`number`): "`___ = H − P`" (H a multiple of 100, 300–900; P 101–H−50, P not a multiple of 10). Answer H−P.
Hint: "Count up from P to the next ten, then to the next hundred, then to H."

### Step 5 — Unknown in another place, and a common mistake
Example slides:
1. `452 − ? = 237`. "Now the unknown is the number taken away. 452 is the whole. 237 and ? are the parts."
2. Bar diagram: whole 452 on top; lower bar 237 with dashed "?". "So `237 + ? = 452`." Count up: `237 + 3 = 240`, `240 + 60 = 300`, `300 + 152 = 452` → `3 + 60 + 152 = 215`.
3. Mistake: "Jo wants `458 − 302` and adds `302 + 458 = 760`." "760 is bigger than 458 — a difference can't be bigger than the whole! Jo should find `302 + ? = 458`. ? = **156**."

Your Turn (`parts`): "`W − ? = R`". `choice` "Which addition equation helps?" `R + ? = W` ✓; `W + R = ?`; `W + ? = R`. `num` "? =" W−R. Generation: W 300–900, R 100–W−80.
Hint: "Which number is the whole? Start at the other number and count up to it."

### Step 6 — "How many more?" with two bars
Example slides:
1. Story: "**268** people visit the zoo on Monday. **541** visit on Saturday. How many more visit on Saturday?"
2. Compare diagram: long bar **541** on top, short bar **268** below, dashed **?** to the end. "The longer bar is the bigger number. The ? is how many **more**."
3. Equations: `541 − 268 = ?` or `268 + ? = 541`. Count up: `268 + 2 = 270`, `+ 30 = 300`, `+ 241 = 541`; `2 + 30 + 241 = 273`.

Your Turn (`parts`): story with two amounts S < L (contexts: cans collected two weeks, runners in two races). Display: compare diagram with blank slots A (long) and B (short). Parts: `num` A = L; `num` B = S; `num` "How many more?" = L−S. Generation: S 150–600, L S+80–950.
Hint: "Put the bigger number in the long bar. The ? is the difference."

`seeIt.reflection`: "How are addition and subtraction related?" Idea: "They use the same three numbers. If 248 + 315 = 563, then 563 − 248 = 315. Subtracting is finding a missing addend."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- **Adding the two numbers** (`302 + 458`) — ask "Can the answer be bigger than the whole?"
- **Putting the whole as an addend** (`563 + ? = 248`) — point to the bar diagram: the whole is the long bar, after the = sign.
- **Counting-up jumps not added together** (child says 263, the last jump) — "Add all your jumps."
- **Across zeros**: writing 400 − 153 = 353 by taking the smaller digit from the bigger in each place — switch to counting up.
- **`452 − ? = 237`** — thinking ? is 452 − 237 is fine (also 215!), but `452 + 237` is wrong.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal:** Use a bar diagram and an addition equation to subtract.
- **Words:** bar diagram, whole, part, addend, unknown, difference, related equations, count up.
- **Demonstrate:** Draw a long bar "563" and a shorter bar "248" under it; dash the gap with "?". Write `563 − 248 = ?` and `248 + ? = 563`. Count up 248 → 250 → 300 → 563; add jumps 2 + 50 + 263 = 315. Check `248 + 315 = 563`.
- **Ask:** "Which number is the whole?" "What do you add to the part to get the whole?" "Is your answer smaller than the whole?" "How can you check with addition?"
- **Checklist:** draws/fills a bar diagram; writes both related equations; counts up correctly including across zeros; solves `452 − ? = 237`; explains why adding the two numbers is wrong; solves how-many-more problems.
