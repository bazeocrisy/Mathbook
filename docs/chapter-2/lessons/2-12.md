## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. Use a **letter** for an unknown (`a`, `b`, `m`, `k`, `t`).
2. Read and fill **part-part-whole** bar diagrams (parts in one bar, bracket for the whole) and **compare / missing-part** diagrams (two bars, dashed piece).
3. Write an equation for a bar diagram, and pick the bar diagram for an equation.
4. Solve two-step problems: add then subtract; subtract twice; "more than" then together; work backward; groups of 100.
5. Solve one-step equations with the letter on either side (`m = 456 − 236`; `867 = 235 + k`).
6. Make sense of a two-step problem (what to find first) and match a story to a diagram.

Prerequisites: 2-9 (bar diagrams, related equations), 2-10, 2-11 (fluent add/subtract within 1,000).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Two-step problem** — a problem you solve with two equations, one after the other.
- **Unknown** — the number we need to find.
- **Letter for the unknown** — a letter like *a* or *b* that stands for the unknown number.
- **Bar diagram** — bars that show how the numbers fit together.
- **Whole / parts** — the parts together make the whole.
- **Equation** — a number sentence with an equal sign, like `246 + 131 = a`.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

Book worked example (Lea): Step 1 `235 + 112 = a` → a = 347; Step 2 `475 − 347 = b` → b = 128. Reasoning: first find what she has (join the two parts), then compare with what she needs (missing part). Each step gets its own bar diagram and its own letter. App Learn uses original numbers: 246 + 131 = a = 377; 500 − 377 = b = 123.

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Six steps. Example numbers: 246, 131, 377, 500, 123, 703, 288, 415, 645, 321, 324, 812, 345, 467, 190, 124, 38, 28, 215, 128, 343, 558, 238, 497, 43, 302, 120, 305, 214, 639. Your Turn never repeats them.

### Step 1 — A letter for the unknown (join two parts)
Example slides:
1. Story: "Max has **246** stickers. Then he gets **131** more. He wants **500** stickers to fill his album." "First: how many stickers does he have?"
2. Diagram (part-part-whole): one bar split 246 \| 131 (widths proportional), dashed bracket **above** labeled ***a***, caption "stickers he has". "We don't know the total yet. Call it ***a***."
3. `246 + 131 = a` → `a = 377`. "Max has 377 stickers."

Your Turn (`parts`): story with two parts p, q (p + q ≤ 900). Display: part-part-whole diagram p \| q with bracket `a`. `choice` "Which equation matches?" [`p + q = a` ✓, `q − p = a`, `a + p = q`]; `num` "a =" p + q. Generation: p, q 3-digit, sum 300–900.
Hint: "The bracket shows the whole. The parts together make the whole."

### Step 2 — The missing part (compare two bars)
Example slides:
1. "He has 377. He needs 500. How many **more** does he need?"
2. Diagram (compare): top bar **377** with a dashed extension labeled ***b*** to the end; bottom bar **500** (full width); caption "stickers needed" over *b*.
3. `500 − 377 = b` (or `377 + b = 500`) → `b = 123`. "Max needs 123 more stickers."

Your Turn (`parts`): whole W, part P shown in a compare diagram with `b`. `multi` "Which equations match?" [`W − P = b` ✓, `P + b = W` ✓, `W + P = b`, `P − W = b`]; `num` "b =" W − P. Generation: W 400–950, P 150–W−40.
Hint: "The dashed piece is what's missing. Subtract, or count up."

### Step 3 — Put the two steps together
Example slides:
1. The whole Max story again with both diagrams side by side (stacked on phones).
2. "Step 1: `246 + 131 = a`, a = 377. Step 2: `500 − a = b` → `500 − 377 = b`, b = 123."
3. "Use the answer from Step 1 in Step 2."

Your Turn (`parts`): generated add-then-subtract story (contexts: shells collected toward a goal, cans collected then given away, steps walked toward a goal). Fields are named after what they hold: `num` "a = (shells) in all now:" (x + y); `num` "b = (shells) still needed:" (T − a) or "b = (cans) left:" (a − T). Each field asks for a named quantity, so both are graded. Generation: x, y 3-digit, x + y between 250 and 800; T either a target above a (T multiple of 50, a + 30 ≤ T ≤ 999 → "how many more needed") or an amount given away below a (multiple of 50, 100 ≤ T ≤ a − 50 → "how many left", b = a − T). Grade both.
Hint: "What do you need to find first?"

### Step 4 — Diagrams and equations
Example slides:
1. "Which bar diagram shows `703 − b = 288`?" Three small diagrams: **A** compare (whole 703 on top; part 288 below with dashed *b*) ✓; **B** one bar 703 \| 288 with bracket *b*; **C** one bar 703 \| *b* with bracket 288. "703 is the whole. 288 is a part. *b* is the other part." → A; `b = 415`.
2. Equation for a diagram: CMP diagram, whole 645 on top, part 321 below with dashed *m*: `645 − 321 = m` (or `321 + m = 645`), `m = 324`.
3. Letter on either side: `812 = 345 + k`. "The equal sign means both sides are the same amount. `345 + k = 812` → `k = 467`."

Your Turn (`parts`): generated equation `W − b = R` or `P + a = W` (random). Display: three diagrams A/B/C, positions shuffled, defined per variant (same as P3/P4):
- **`W − b = R`**: ✓ CMP whole W on top / part R below / dashed *b*; ✗ PPW W \| R with bracket *b*; ✗ PPW W \| *b* with bracket R.
- **`P + a = W`**: ✓ PPW P \| *a* with bracket W; ✗ PPW P \| W with bracket *a*; ✗ CMP whole *a* on top / part P / dashed W.
- Rule: no distractor may be a valid picture of the equation (e.g. CMP whole W / part P / dashed *a* is a correct picture of `P + a = W` and must never be offered as wrong).
`choice` "Which diagram matches?" (Diagram A/B/C); `num` letter value. Generation: W 300–950; R or P 100–(W − 60); letter value = W − R or W − P (≥ 60).
Hint: "Find the whole first. Is it a number or the letter?"

### Step 5 — Different kinds of two-step stories
Example slides (one per kind; each with its diagram(s)):
1. **Spend twice**: "Sam has **$190**. He buys a ball for **$124** and a pump for **$38**. How much is left?" `190 − 124 = a` (66), `a − 38 = b` (28). Or add the costs first: `124 + 38 = 162`, `190 − 162 = 28`. "Both orders work."
2. **More than, then together**: "Amy has **215** cards. Raj has **128 more** than Amy. How many together?" Compare diagram: Amy bar 215; Raj bar 215 + dashed 128. `215 + 128 = r` (343), then `215 + 343 = t` (558).
3. **Work backward**: "A school garden picks **238** carrots in the morning and more in the afternoon. The class eats **497** carrots and has **43** left. How many were picked in the afternoon?" Picked in all = `497 + 43 = 540`; afternoon `540 − 238 = 302`. (Another order also works: `497 − 238 = 259`, `259 + 43 = 302`.)
4. **Groups of 100**: "A shop has **8** boxes of 100 crayons. It sells **2** boxes, then **3** boxes. How many crayons are left?" Boxes left `8 − 2 − 3 = 3` → **300** crayons (or 800 − 500 = 300).

Your Turn (`parts`): random kind among the three below. Fields are named after what they hold; where more than one order is valid, only the final answer is asked and graded.
- **Spend twice** — "N has $M. N buys a ___ for $c1 and a ___ for $c2. How much is left?" One field: `num` "Money left: $" = M − c1 − c2. Generation: M 150–600; c1 50–(M − 70); c2 10–99; c1 + c2 ≤ M − 10; c1 ≠ c2.
- **More than, then together** — "N1 has x ___. N2 has d more than N1. How many together?" Fields: `num` "N2 has:" = x + d; `num` "Together:" = 2x + d. Generation: x 100–400; d 50–250; 2x + d ≤ 999.
- **Work backward** — "A ___ makes m in the morning and more in the afternoon. It sells s and has l left. How many in the afternoon?" One field: `num` "Afternoon:" = s + l − m. Generation: m 150–350; s 300–600; l 20–90; s + l ≤ 999; (s + l) − m ≥ 50.
Hint: "Say the story in your own words. What do you find first?"

### Step 6 — Make sense of a two-step problem
Example slides:
1. Diagram: one bar 120 \| 305 \| 214 with bracket *t*. "What story could this be?" "A class picked 120 apples on Monday, 305 on Tuesday, and 214 on Wednesday. How many in all?" `120 + 305 = 425`, `425 + 214 = 639`, `t = 639`.
2. "To make sense of a two-step problem: read it, say what you know and what you need, draw a bar diagram, write an equation for each step with a letter, then check that the answer makes sense."

Your Turn (`parts`): generated 3-part diagram x \| y \| z with bracket `t` (each 100–350, **x + y + z ≤ 999**). `choice` "Which story fits?" [✓ three amounts added for a total; ✗ start with y, take away x and z; ✗ how many more y than x]; `num` "t =".
Hint: "The bracket covers all the parts. What do we do to find the whole?"

`seeIt.reflection`: "How can you make sense of a two-step problem?" Idea: "Find what the question asks. Decide what you need to know first. Draw a bar diagram and use a letter for each unknown."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- **Doing only one step** (answering 383 instead of 117) — "Did you answer the question that was asked?"
- **Adding when the story compares** (`500 + 377`) — use the compare diagram: the dashed piece is missing.
- **"More than" read as the total** (Raj = 128) — Raj has Amy's amount **and** 128 more.
- **Work backward**: subtracting 43 from 497 instead of adding — "Eaten and left together are everything that was picked."
- **Groups of 100**: answering in boxes (3) instead of crayons (300).
- **Letter on the right side** (`812 = 345 + k`) — read it from right to left; the equal sign means "same as".

## 13. New interaction or model needed

### Bar diagram renderer — `pv.barDiagramHTML(spec)` (shared by 2-9 and 2-12; display only, SVG or HTML/CSS)

Two layouts, matching the book:

1. **`kind: 'ppw'` (part-part-whole)** — one horizontal bar split into 2–3 segments.
   - `parts: [label, label, (label)]`, `sizes: [n, n, (n)]` (true values, used only for proportional widths, minimum segment width ≈ 18% so labels fit).
   - `whole: label` drawn as a dashed bracket (|-----label-----|) spanning the full bar, `bracket: 'above' | 'below'`.
   - Optional `caption` over the bracket (e.g. "stickers he has").
2. **`kind: 'cmp'` (compare / missing part)** — two left-aligned bars.
   - `long: label` (full width), `short: label` (width = short/long), `gap: label` drawn as a dashed bracket from the end of the short bar to the end of the long bar, level with the short bar.
   - `order: 'longTop' | 'shortTop'` (2-9 Learn and book #2 put the whole on top; 2-12 Learn Step 2 puts the short bar on top).
   - Optional `caption` over the gap (e.g. "stickers needed").

`label` is one of: a number (formatted with commas), a letter (italic, e.g. `{ letter: 'a' }`), `'?'`, or a **blank slot** `{ slot: 'A' }` drawn as an empty dashed box with a small tag "A" (the child fills it through an ordinary `num` part labelled "Box A …"). Sizes stay correct even when the label is a letter or slot.

Requirements: theme-aware fill (site palette, readable in both themes), an `aria-label` describing the diagram in words (e.g. "Bar diagram: parts 246 and 131, whole a"), fits 320 px wide (labels never overflow; numbers ≤ 3 digits plus letters), and a compact size option `small: true` for the A/B/C choice displays (three diagrams stacked vertically on phones, each headed "Diagram A", "Diagram B", "Diagram C").

### No new question type
- "Fill in the bar diagram" = `parts` with `num` fields + a diagram with slots in `display`.
- "Draw a bar diagram for an equation" = three small diagrams in `display` + `choice` "Diagram A/B/C".
- "Write an equation" = `choice` or `multi` among equations.
- Letters in equations: plain italic text; answers entered as numbers in `num` parts labelled "a =".

## 14. Open questions / assumptions

- Book #3/#4 ask the child to **draw** a diagram; the app converts this to choosing among three diagrams (auto-gradable). A free-draw tool is out of scope.
- Book #12 asks the child to **write** a story; the app asks which story fits (auto-graded) plus an `explain` item in Practice Together for the child to tell their own.
- Grading of steps: every step field is named after the quantity it holds. Spend-twice and work-backward items ask for and grade only the final answer, because more than one order is valid. Add-then-subtract and more-than-together items grade both fields, because each field asks for a named quantity (e.g. "money he has now").
- Book #11 starts with 10 boxes = 1,000; the app keeps totals below 1,000 (9 or fewer boxes) to stay inside "within 1,000" for the child's input; adjust if the builder wants 1,000 included.
