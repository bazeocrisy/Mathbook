Spec for the builder. Quick practice of 2-digit addition and subtraction with a "use a 10" strategy and number-line jumps, then a short check. All items are original.

## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goal: *I can add and subtract 2-digit numbers quickly by making a ten, and I can write an equation for number-line jumps.*

- Make a 10 to add: move ones from one addend to the other (add to one, take the same from the other) — the sum stays the same.
- Use a 10 to subtract: change **both** numbers the same way so the number you subtract becomes a ten — the difference stays the same.
- Read jumps on an open number line and write one equation (start ± total jump = end).
- Recall facts with 0, 1, 2 instantly.

Prerequisites: 2-8 (adjust numbers), 2-10/2-11 (fluency within 1,000).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Make a 10** — change a number so it ends in 0 (49 → 50).
- **Adjust** — change the numbers in a way that keeps the answer the same.
- **Jump** — a move along a number line; + jumps go right, − jumps go left.
- **Equation** — a number sentence with an = sign, like 26 + 45 = 71.

## 4. Methods taught

1. **Make a 10 to add.** 39 + 24: 39 needs 1 to make 40. Take 1 from 24 → 40 + 23 = 63. Why: you moved 1 from one addend to the other, so the total is the same.
2. **Use a 10 to subtract.** 67 − 32: 32 is 2 more than 30. Take 2 from both → 65 − 30 = 35. Why: taking the same amount from both numbers keeps the distance between them the same. (Extension in the parent guide: 72 − 29 → add 1 to both → 73 − 30 = 43.)
3. **Number-line jumps → equation.** Jumps of +3 then +45 from 37 land on 85, so the total jump is 48: 37 + 48 = 85. For left jumps from 94: −4 then −50 lands on 40, total −54: 94 − 54 = 40.
4. **Difference between the two** (Fluency Talk): adding — change the two numbers in *opposite* ways (+1 and −1); subtracting — change both the *same* way (−3 and −3, or +1 and +1).

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Three steps (Example → Your Turn). Demo numbers: 39 + 24, 58 + 27, 67 − 32, 93 − 51, 37 + 48 (jumps), 94 − 54 (jumps).

### Step 1 · Make a 10 to add
Example slides (adjust-arrow model, §13 B):
1. "39 + 24. Which number is close to a ten? 39 — it needs **1** more to make 40."
2. Arrows: 39 **+1** → 40; 24 **−1** → 23. "We gave 1 from 24 to 39."
3. "40 + 23 = **63**. So 39 + 24 = 63."
4. "Try 58 + 27: 58 needs 2. 60 + 25 = **85**."

Your Turn (`parts`): a in {x8, x9} (x = 1–7), b 2-digit with ones digit ≥ the amount a needs, a + b ≤ 99, and a + b needs regrouping. Parts: `num` "How many does {a} need to make a ten?" · `num` "New equation: {a+k} + ___" (= b − k) · `num` sum. Hint: "Give some of the second number to the first one."

### Step 2 · Use a 10 to subtract
Example slides:
1. "67 − 32. The number we subtract is 32. It is **2** more than 30."
2. Arrows: 67 **−2** → 65; 32 **−2** → 30. "Take 2 from **both** numbers."
3. "65 − 30 = **35**. So 67 − 32 = 35."
4. "Why both? If you and a friend both step back 2 steps, you're still the same distance apart."
5. "Try 93 − 51: take 1 from both → 92 − 50 = **42**."

Your Turn (`parts`): b 2-digit with ones digit 1–4, a 2-digit with ones digit ≥ b's ones digit (no regrouping, as in the book), a > b. Parts: `num` "Take ___ from both" (= b's ones digit) · `num` "{a − k} − {b − k} =" answer. Hint: "Make the number you subtract a ten. Do the same to the other number."

### Step 3 · Number-line jumps
Example slides (jump-line model, §13 A):
1. Line with 37, 40, 85; arc +3 from 37 to 40, arc +45 from 40 to 85. "We started at 37 and jumped right 3, then 45."
2. "Total jump: 3 + 45 = 48. Equation: **37 + 48 = 85**."
3. Line with marks 40, 90, 94; arc −4 from 94 to 90, then arc −50 from 90 to 40. "Start at 94. Jump left 4, then 50. Total 54. **94 − 54 = 40**."

Your Turn (`parts`): random add or subtract. Add: start s (2-digit, not a multiple of 10), first jump to the next ten, second jump a 2-digit number; end ≤ 99. Subtract: start s (2-digit), first jump back to the ten below, second jump back a multiple of 10; end ≥ 10. Parts: `num` start · `choice` + / − · `num` total jump · `num` end (the line shows start and end labels, so this checks reading and the total jump). Hint: "Add the two jumps to get one jump. Right is +, left is −."

Learn reflection (talk, never graded): "How is making a 10 different when you subtract than when you add?" Idea: "When I add, I give from one number to the other (+1 and −1). When I subtract, I change both numbers the same way (−3 and −3)."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Adding to both numbers when adding (39 + 24 → 40 + 25). Fix: "give, don't make new."
- Changing only one number when subtracting (67 − 32 → 67 − 30 = 37). Fix: "both step back the same."
- Writing the second jump instead of the total jump (26 + 41 = 71). Fix: add the jumps first.
- Wrong direction on the line (+ for left jumps). Fix: right is bigger.
- Facts with 0: 84 + 0 = 0 or 840. Fix: adding 0 changes nothing.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal**: quick 2-digit addition and subtraction using a ten; number-line equations.
- **Demonstrate**: 39 + 24 with coins or base-ten pieces: move 1 one to make 4 tens. 67 − 32 on a ruler: slide both ends back 2 — the gap stays the same.
- **Ask**: "Which number is close to a ten?" "How many does it need?" "Do you change the numbers the same way or the opposite way?" "What is the total jump?"
- **Extension**: "add to both" for subtraction: 72 − 29 → 73 − 30 = 43.
- **Checklist**: makes a 10 to add · uses a 10 to subtract · writes an equation for jumps · knows 0/1/2 facts instantly · explains the difference between adjusting for + and −.

## 13. New interaction or model needed

**A. Jump number line (static)** — shared with any lesson that uses open number lines (2-7, 2-9, 2-11 may want it too):
`jumpLineHTML({ marks: [37, 40, 85], jumps: [{ from: 37, to: 40, label: '+3' }, { from: 40, to: 85, label: '+45' }] })`. An open number line (no scale ticks needed; positions proportional), a tick and label at each mark, and an arc above the line for each jump with an arrowhead at `to` and the label above the arc. Right jumps and left jumps use two different colours. Labels never overlap (short arcs get labels nudged up). Accessible label: "Number line: from 37 jump +3 to 40, then +45 to 85."

**B. Adjust arrows (static)** — for Learn 1–2 and explanations:
`adjustHTML({ a: 39, b: 24, op: '+', da: +1, db: -1 })` renders the equation "39 + 24 = ?", a small coloured box under each number ("+1", "−1") with a down arrow, and the new equation "40 + 23 = 63" below. (Same visual as 2-8 might need — share if 2-8 defines it.)

**C. Equation-with-blanks layout** — "___ + ___ = ___" as inline `num` parts in one line. If `parts` can only stack labels vertically, use three labeled parts ("Start", "Jump", "End") — acceptable.

No timer is required.

## 14. Open questions / assumptions

- Placement: "Fluency" as its own item after the Unit Review in Math Lessons, or inside the Unit Review menu. Suggest its own small lesson ("Fluency Practice") with Learn · Practice · Fluency Check · My Results.
- Timing: the book calls this "fluency" but shows no timer. I recommend showing elapsed time only (no countdown, no pressure).
- Subtraction adjusting is taught only "down" (book); the "up" version is a parent-guide extension.
