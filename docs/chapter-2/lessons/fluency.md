# Unit 2 Fluency Practice and Fluency Check (pages 99–100)

Spec for the builder. Quick practice of 2-digit addition and subtraction with a "use a 10" strategy and number-line jumps, then a short check. All items are original.

## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goal: *I can add and subtract 2-digit numbers quickly by making a ten, and I can write an equation for number-line jumps.*

- Make a 10 to add: move ones from one addend to the other (add to one, take the same from the other) — the sum stays the same.
- Use a 10 to subtract: change **both** numbers the same way so the number you subtract becomes a ten — the difference stays the same.
- Read jumps on an open number line and write one equation (start ± total jump = end).
- Recall facts with 0, 1, 2 instantly.

Prerequisites: 2-8 (adjust numbers), 2-10/2-11 (fluency within 1,000).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Make a 10** — change a number so it ends in 0 (49 → 50).
- **Adjust** — change the numbers in a way that keeps the answer the same.
- **Jump** — a move along a number line; + jumps go right, − jumps go left.
- **Equation** — a number sentence with an = sign, like 26 + 45 = 71.

## 4. Methods taught

1. **Make a 10 to add.** 39 + 24: 39 needs 1 to make 40. Take 1 from 24 → 40 + 23 = 63. Why: you moved 1 from one addend to the other, so the total is the same.
2. **Use a 10 to subtract.** 67 − 32: 32 is 2 more than 30. Take 2 from both → 65 − 30 = 35. Why: taking the same amount from both numbers keeps the distance between them the same. (Extension in the parent guide: 72 − 29 → add 1 to both → 73 − 30 = 43.)
3. **Number-line jumps → equation.** Jumps of +3 then +45 from 37 land on 85, so the total jump is 48: 37 + 48 = 85. Left jumps come in two patterns, and both are taught: **to the ten below, then tens** (94 −4 → 90, −50 → 40: 94 − 54 = 40), and the book's **ones first, then tens** (86 −4 → 82, −30 → 52: 86 − 34 = 52), which does not land on a ten.
4. **Difference between the two** (Fluency Talk): adding — change the two numbers in *opposite* ways (+1 and −1); subtracting — change both the *same* way (−3 and −3, or +1 and +1).

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Three steps (Example → Your Turn). Demo numbers: 39 + 24, 58 + 27, 67 − 32, 93 − 51, 37 + 48 (jumps), 94 − 54 (jumps), 86 − 34 (jumps).

### Step 1 · Make a 10 to add
Example slides (F1 arrow equation with `tags`):
1. "39 + 24. Which number is close to a ten? 39 — it needs **1** more to make 40."
2. Arrows: 39 **+1** → 40; 24 **−1** → 23. "We gave 1 from 24 to 39."
3. "40 + 23 = **63**. So 39 + 24 = 63."
4. "Try 58 + 27: 58 needs 2. 60 + 25 = **85**."

Your Turn (A2 `chain`, preset `adjust`, `keep: 'sum'`): a in {x8, x9} (x = 1–7), b 2-digit with ones digit ≥ the amount a needs, a + b ≤ 99, and a + b needs regrouping. Row 1 printed "{a} + {b} = ?"; row 2 `[new a] + [new b] = [answer]`, with live tags on the arrows in guided mode. Graded: the new pair keeps the sum and at least one number changed (Decision 10); answer = a + b exactly. If neither new number ends in 0, the child gets the gentle tip "Try making {a} a ten." (not a wrong mark). Nothing on screen shows the new numbers before the child types them. Hint: "Give some of the second number to the first one."

### Step 2 · Use a 10 to subtract
Example slides (F1 with `tags`):
1. "67 − 32. The number we subtract is 32. It is **2** more than 30."
2. Arrows: 67 **−2** → 65; 32 **−2** → 30. "Take 2 from **both** numbers."
3. "65 − 30 = **35**. So 67 − 32 = 35."
4. "Why both? If you and a friend both step back 2 steps, you're still the same distance apart."
5. "Try 93 − 51: take 1 from both → 92 − 50 = **42**."

Your Turn (A2 `chain`, preset `adjust`, `keep: 'diff'`): b 2-digit with ones digit 1–4, a 2-digit with ones digit ≥ b's ones digit (no regrouping, as in the book), a > b. Row 1 printed "{a} − {b} = ?"; row 2 `[new a] − [new b] = [answer]`. Graded: same difference and at least one number changed; answer exact. If the child changes the numbers in opposite ways, the message is "You changed them opposite ways. In subtracting, change both the same way." If the new number being subtracted doesn't end in 0, gentle tip only. Hint: "Make the number you subtract a ten. Do the same to the other number."

### Step 3 · Number-line jumps
Example slides (F5 number line, `open: true`, `hops`):
1. Marks 37, 40, 85; hop +3 from 37 to 40, hop +45 from 40 to 85. "We started at 37 and jumped right 3, then 45."
2. "Total jump: 3 + 45 = 48. Equation: **37 + 48 = 85**."
3. Marks 40, 90, 94; hop −4 from 94 to 90, then −50 from 90 to 40. "Start at 94. Jump left 4, then 50. Total 54. **94 − 54 = 40**."
4. Marks 52, 82, 86; hop −4 from 86 to 82, then −30 from 82 to 52. "Here the first jump takes away the ones, and we land on 82, not on a ten. Total 4 + 30 = 34. **86 − 34 = 52**."

Your Turn (A2 `chain`, preset `frame`): random add or subtract. The line shows every mark and hop label; the child writes one equation in `[ ] + [ ] = [ ]` or `[ ] − [ ] = [ ]` (the operator is printed, matching the hop direction).
- Add: start s (2-digit, **not a multiple of 10**), first hop to the next ten, second hop a 2-digit number; end ≤ 99.
- Subtract (half of the items each): (a) start s (2-digit, **not a multiple of 10**), first hop back to the ten below, second hop back a multiple of 10; or (b) the book's form: start s (not a multiple of 10), first hop −k with 1 ≤ k < s's ones digit (so it does **not** land on a ten), second hop back a multiple of 10. End ≥ 10 in both.
- **Grading rule (Decision 15):** the equation must match the line. Addition: start + total hop = end, with the two addends in **either order** (`commute`), e.g. 18 + 58 = 76 or 58 + 18 = 76. Subtraction: only start − total hop = end (no commute); a true equation that does not describe the hops, such as 76 − 30 = 46, is marked wrong. All three numbers must be filled.
- Hint: "Add the two jumps to get one jump. Right is +, left is −."

Learn reflection (talk, never graded): "How is making a 10 different when you subtract than when you add?" Idea: "When I add, I give from one number to the other (+1 and −1). When I subtract, I change both numbers the same way (−3 and −3)."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Adding to both numbers when adding (39 + 24 → 40 + 25). Fix: "give, don't make new."
- Changing only one number when subtracting (67 − 32 → 67 − 30 = 37). Fix: "both step back the same."
- Writing the second jump instead of the total jump (26 + 41 = 71). Fix: add the jumps first.
- Writing a true equation that doesn't match the line (76 − 30 = 46). Fix: start where the line starts; the jump is the total of the hops.
- Wrong direction on the line (+ for left jumps). Fix: right is bigger.
- Facts with 0: 84 + 0 = 0 or 840. Fix: adding 0 changes nothing.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal**: quick 2-digit addition and subtraction using a ten; number-line equations. Accuracy with a good strategy — no timing.
- **Demonstrate**: 39 + 24 with coins or base-ten pieces: move 1 one to make 4 tens. 67 − 32 on a ruler: slide both ends back 2 — the gap stays the same. (Book's Fluency Talk example, for reference: 28 + 17 → 30 + 15 = 45.)
- **Ask**: "Which number is close to a ten?" "How many does it need?" "Do you change the numbers the same way or the opposite way?" "What is the total jump?"
- **Extension**: "add to both" for subtraction: 72 − 29 → 73 − 30 = 43.
- **Checklist**: makes a 10 to add · uses a 10 to subtract · writes an equation for jumps (both subtraction patterns) · knows 0/1/2 facts instantly · explains the difference between adjusting for + and −.
