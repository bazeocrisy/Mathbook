## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. Subtract by **decomposing the number being subtracted** (hundreds, tens, ones; split the ones to land on a ten when helpful).
2. Subtract by **adjusting both numbers by the same amount** so the number being subtracted becomes a ten or hundred (the difference stays the same).
3. Subtract with a **related addition equation** (count up).
4. Decide that more than one strategy works; choose an efficient one; find the error in "decompose the bigger number and subtract from the smaller".
5. Word problems: "fewer", "how many more", reading a table, extra information, two subtractions and comparing the result to 100.

Prerequisites: 2-7 (decompose to subtract), 2-8 (adjust numbers), 2-9 (use addition to subtract).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Difference** — the answer to a subtraction problem.
- **Decompose** — break a number into parts (145 → 100 + 40 + 5).
- **Adjust** — change a number to make it easier. In subtraction, change **both** numbers by the **same** amount.
- **Related addition equation** — an addition equation with the same numbers (`145 + ? = 562`).
- **Efficient** — quick and easy for you, with few steps.
- **Fewer** — less; "145 fewer" means take 145 away.
- **Extra information** — a number in a story you do not need.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

Book worked example: `543 − 134 = 409`, three ways.
1. **Decompose one number** — take away 100, then 30, then 3 (to reach 410), then 1. Why: taking away the parts one at a time takes away the whole number.
2. **Adjust numbers** — add 6 to both: `549 − 140`. Why: moving both numbers up the same amount keeps the distance (difference) between them the same. (Note: for subtraction both change the same way; for addition one goes up and one goes down.)
3. **Related addition equation** — `134 + ? = 543`; count up.
4. **Choose** — all three work; pick what is efficient (e.g. adjust when the number subtracted is close to a ten/hundred; count up when the numbers are close together or across zeros).
5. **Extra information** — Sunday's 248 tickets are not needed to find Saturday.

App Learn uses original numbers: `562 − 145 = 417`.

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Five steps. Example numbers: 562, 145, 417, 567, 150, 600, 359, 241, 599, 358, 357, 124, 233, 363, 130, 236. Your Turn never repeats them. Final answers are graded; any strategy is accepted unless the step asks for specific intermediate numbers.

### Step 1 — Decompose the number you take away
Example slides:
1. Story: "A school library lent **562** books in March. In April it lent **145 fewer** than in March. How many books did it lend in April?" "Fewer means subtract: `562 − 145`."
2. "Break 145 into 100 + 40 + 5. Take away one part at a time."
3. `562 − 100 = 462` → `462 − 40 = 422`.
4. "5 ones: take 2 first to land on a ten: `422 − 2 = 420`, then `420 − 3 = 417`." **562 − 145 = 417**.

Your Turn (`parts`): "Find `a − b` by taking away the hundreds, then the tens, then the ones." Parts: `num` "a − H =" , `num` "then − T =", `num` "then − O =" (the final difference). Generation: a 400–989, b 111–a−100 with hundreds, tens and ones digits of b all non-zero; ones digit of b ≤ ones digit of a in half the cases (no ten crossed) and greater in the other half. Grade all three.
Hint: "First take away the hundreds of b. Then the tens. Then the ones."

### Step 2 — Adjust both numbers
Example slides:
1. "`562 − 145`. 145 is 5 away from 150."
2. Adjust arrows (§13): **+5** under 562, **+5** under 145 → `567 − 150`.
3. `567 − 150 = 417`. "Add the same amount to **both** numbers. The difference stays the same — like sliding both ends of a ruler."
4. Across zeros: `600 − 359`. Take 1 from **both**: `599 − 358 = 241`. "No regrouping needed!"

Your Turn (`parts`): "Add the same amount to both numbers so `b` becomes a whole ten. Then subtract." Parts: a **keep-the-difference pair** (2-10 §13 item 3, `op: '−'`) "a becomes:" / "b becomes:", then `num` "Difference:". Expected pair: a + k, b + k. Grading (Decision 10): the pair is correct for **any** whole numbers x ≥ y with x − y = a − b and (x, y) ≠ (a, b) (e.g. taking the same amount from both is also accepted); the difference must equal a − b exactly. Generation: a 300–990; b 106–(a − 40) with ones digit 6–9 (k = 10 − ones digit, 1–4); a + k ≤ 999.
Hint: "How much does b need to reach the next ten? Add that much to a, too."

### Step 3 — Use a related addition equation
Example slides:
1. "`562 − 145 = ?` is the same as `145 + ? = 562`."
2. Count up: `145 + 5 = 150` → `150 + 50 = 200` → `200 + 362 = 562`. Jumps `5 + 50 + 362 = 417`.
3. "Check: `145 + 417 = 562` ✓."

Your Turn (`parts`): `choice` "Which addition equation is related to `a − b = ?`" [`b + ? = a` ✓, `a + b = ?`, `a + ? = b`]; `num` "? =". Generation: a 300–950, b 100–a−50, includes a multiple of 100 for a in about a third of cases.
Hint: "Start at b and count up to a."

### Step 4 — More than one way (choose a strategy)
Example slides:
1. "Is decomposing the **only** way to find `357 − 124`?" Decompose: `357 − 100 = 257`, `− 20 = 237`, `− 4 = 233`.
2. Adjust: `+6` both → `363 − 130 = 233`. Count up: `124 + ? = 357` → 233. "No! Three ways, same answer."
3. Two children solve `173 − 138`: one adjusts to `175 − 140`; one writes `138 + ? = 173`. Both get **35**. "Pick the one that is quick for you."
4. Mistake: "Ray says: to do `357 − 124`, decompose 357 and take the parts away from 124." "That's backward! We take 124 away **from 357**. Decompose the number you take away (124)."

Your Turn (`parts`): `multi` "Which ways give the right answer for `a − b`?" [`(a+k) − (b+k)` ✓, `a − H − T − O` (written with b's parts) ✓, `b + ? = a` ✓, `a − (b+k)` (only one adjusted) ✗, "decompose a and subtract its parts from b" ✗]; `num` "a − b =". Generation: b ones digit 7–9, k = 10 − ones digit; a − b ≥ 100.
Hint: "When you adjust, change both numbers. Always take away from the bigger number."

### Step 5 — Word problems: compare, tables, extra information, two steps
Example slides:
1. Table (road sign): Elm Town 124 · Lake City 217 · Hill Park 352 miles. "How much farther is Hill Park than Elm Town?" `352 − 124 = 228`. "Farther than means compare — subtract."
2. Extra information: "Jen scored **286** points. That was **95** more than last time. Ali scored **431**. How many more did Ali score than Jen?" "We need 431 and 286. The 95 is extra." `431 − 286 = 145`.
3. Two steps: "Kai has **600** points. He spends **275** and then **210**. Does he have more than 100 left?" `600 − 275 = 325`, `325 − 210 = 115`. "115 > 100, so yes."

Your Turn (`parts`): table with three places (generated distances 100–450, increasing). `num` "How much farther is the last place than the first?" (last − first). Second part: `choice` yes/no for a two-step "more than 100 left?" with total T (multiples of 50, 500–900), spends s1, s2 (3-digit), remainder r between 20 and 200 and ≠ 100; answer "Yes" iff r > 100. `num` "Left:" r.
Hint: "Farther means subtract the smaller distance. For two spends, subtract one, then the other."

`seeIt.reflection`: "How do you decide if a subtraction strategy is efficient?" Idea: "It has few steps and easy numbers. If the number taken away is close to a ten or hundred, adjust. If the numbers are close together, count up."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- **Adjusting in opposite directions** (562 + 5 and 145 − 5) — that is the addition rule; in subtraction move **both the same way**.
- **Adjusting only one number** (`652 − 120` for `652 − 117`) — answer off by 3.
- **Subtracting from the wrong number** / smaller-from-larger in each place (`153 − 79` → 126).
- **Smaller-from-larger in the ones**: `422 − 5` done as 423 (taking 2 from 5 instead of 5 from 2).
- **Using extra information** — "Which two numbers does the question compare?"
- **Two-step**: stopping after one subtraction.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal:** Subtract within 1,000 with a strategy that is efficient for your child.
- **Words:** difference, decompose, adjust, related addition equation, efficient, fewer, extra information.
- **Demonstrate:** `562 − 145` three ways: (1) − 100, − 40, − 2, − 3 → 417; (2) + 5 to both → 567 − 150 = 417; (3) 145 + ? = 562 by counting up 5 + 50 + 362 = 417. (Book example: 543 − 134 = 409.)
- **Ask:** "Which number are we taking away?" "Is it close to a ten or hundred?" "If you change one number, what must you do to the other?" "Which numbers do we need?" "Is it more than 100?"
- **Checklist:** decomposes the number subtracted; adjusts both numbers the same way; uses related addition; recognizes valid and invalid strategies; reads a table; ignores extra information; solves two-step comparisons with 100.
