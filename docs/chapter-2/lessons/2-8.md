## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. See that different-looking sums can be equal when one addend goes down and the other goes up by the same amount.
2. **Adjust addition:** take an amount from one addend and give it to the other (sum unchanged).
3. **Adjust subtraction:** add the same amount to both numbers, or subtract the same amount from both (difference unchanged); show it on a number line (the gap keeps its length).
4. Choose a helpful adjustment (make a ten or a hundred) and explain why it is easier.
5. Spot wrong adjustments (same direction in addition; opposite directions in subtraction) and fix an answer when only one number was adjusted.
6. Solve "how many more" comparison stories by adjusting.

Prerequisites: adding/subtracting multiples of 10 and 100; 2-6 and 2-7 strategies; difference as distance on a number line.

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Adjust** — change a number a little to make it easier to work with.
- **Friendly number** — a number that is easy to add or subtract, like 200 or 450.
- **Sum** — the answer when you add.
- **Difference** — the answer when you subtract. It is also the distance between two numbers on a number line.
- **Addend** — a number you add.
- **Equation** — a number sentence with an equal sign, like 234 + 200 = 434.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

1. **Equal sums look different** (Be Curious): moving 1 or more from one addend to the other keeps the total.
2. **Adjust addition — opposite moves:** subtract k from one addend, add k to the other. Book: 224 + 109 → 223 + 110 = 333.
3. **Adjust subtraction — same moves:** add k to both or subtract k from both. Book: 333 − 212 → 331 − 210 = 121. Number line: sliding both ends the same amount keeps the distance.
4. **Addition vs subtraction structure** (Math is… Structure): addition → change in opposite directions; subtraction → change in the same direction.
5. **Choosing the adjustment:** aim to make the number you add/subtract a ten or hundred (… 9 → +1, … 8 → +2; …1 / …2 → subtract).
6. **Fixing a one-number adjustment** (12, Reflect): if only one number changes, the answer changes by that amount, so undo it (adding 1 less → add 1 back; subtracting 1 more → add 1 back, etc.).

Five steps. Demo numbers (Your Turn avoids): 46 + 29, 45 + 30, 50 + 25, 48 + 27, 236 + 198, 452 − 197, 568 − 323, 367 + 196, 645 + 298, 625 − 197, 815 − 768, 434 − 214, and the book's 224 + 109, 333 − 212, 457 + 208, 457 + 251.

### Step 1 — Same sum, different numbers
Example parts:
1. Four cards: **46 + 29 · 45 + 30 · 50 + 25 · 48 + 27**. "Which doesn't belong?" (any reason is fine — e.g. 45 + 30 and 50 + 25 have a 0 in the ones).
2. "Surprise: they all equal **75**!"
3. "46 + 29 → 45 + 30: one moved from 46 to 29. The total stays 75." (adjust-arrows −1, +1, M-ADJUST visual)
Your Turn: `parts` `multi`: "Which have the same sum as a + b? Choose all." a in 30–69, b in 15–39 with b ending in 7–9; six choices: three correct (a−k, b+k for three different k, including one that makes b a ten), three wrong (a+k, b+k; a−k, b−k; a+1, b). Hint: "If one goes down and the other goes up the same amount, the sum stays the same." Explanation lists each sum.

### Step 2 — Adjust addition
Example parts:
1. "236 + 198. 198 is close to 200."
2. Arrows: 236 **−2**, 198 **+2** → **234 + 200 = 434**. "Take 2 from one addend. Give 2 to the other."
3. "Check: 236 + 198 = 434 too."
Your Turn: M-ADJUST (§13), addition: a 3-digit 120–799, b 3-digit with ones digit 7, 8 or 9 (b ≤ 999 − a), a's ones ≥ 10 − (b's ones). Child types the new numbers and the sum. Accept **any** valid adjustment (§13). Hint: "What can you add to b to make a ten? Take that much from the other number."

### Step 3 — Adjust subtraction (and see it on a number line)
Example parts:
1. "452 − 197. Make 197 into 200."
2. Arrows: 452 **+3**, 197 **+3** → **455 − 200 = 255**. "In subtraction, do the **same** thing to both numbers."
3. Two number lines (existing `numberLineHTML`, stacked with `slide-pair`): band 197→452 and band 200→455, both labelled "255 apart". "Both ends slid 3 to the right. The gap did not change."
4. "You can also take away the same amount: 568 − 323 → (−3, −3) → **565 − 320 = 245**."
Your Turn: M-ADJUST subtraction: a 3-digit 300–999, b 3-digit with ones digit 7–9 (or 1–2, 50/50), a − b ≥ 50. Accept any valid adjustment. Then `choice` "How is adjusting subtraction different from adjusting addition?" → "In subtraction I change both numbers the same way. In addition I change them opposite ways." (wrong: "There is no difference." · "In subtraction I change only one number." · "In subtraction I change them opposite ways.") Hint: "Add (or take away) the same amount from both numbers."

### Step 4 — Spot and fix mistakes
Example parts:
1. "Leo adds **367 + 196**. He changes them to 370 + 199 = 569. He added 3 to **both**. That makes the sum 6 too big. Right way: 363 + 200 = **563**."
2. "Kim adds **645 + 298**. She changes only 298 to 300 and gets 945. She added 2 extra, so take 2 away: **943**."
3. "Ann finds **625 − 197**. She subtracts 200 instead and gets 425. She took away 3 too many, so **add 3 back**: **428**. In subtraction, the fix goes the other way!"
Your Turn: generated `parts`, addition or subtraction (50/50). Addition: a + b (b ending in 8 or 9); a child "changes only b to the next ten" and shows R = a + (b + k); key "Subtract k". Subtraction: a − b (a 300–999, b ending in 7–9, a − b ≥ 50); the child subtracts the next ten (b + k) instead and shows R = a − (b + k); key "Add k". `choice` "What should she do to fix the answer?" (real numbers; wrong choices: the opposite direction ("Add k" / "Subtract k"), "Nothing — it's right", and the right direction with 2k); `num` "Correct answer" → a + b or a − b. Hint: "Did she add (or take away) too much or too little?"

### Step 5 — Story problems
Example parts:
1. "A library had 768 visitors in March and 815 in April. How many more in April? 815 − 768 → (+2, +2) → 817 − 770 = **47**."
2. Two-step story (book Learn idea, new numbers): "236 people are in the red tent and 214 in the blue tent. Then 198 more people come to the red tent. How many more people are in the red tent than the blue tent?" First add: 236 + 198 → (−2, +2) → 234 + 200 = **434**. Then subtract: 434 − 214 → (−14, −14) → 420 − 200 = **220**. "Adding: opposite moves. Subtracting: same moves."
Your Turn: generated "how many more" story (contexts: orders, points, steps, tickets): bigger 3-digit, smaller 3-digit with ones 7–9, difference 20–200. `parts`: M-ADJUST subtraction + `num` answer with unit. Hint: "How many more → subtract."

Learn reflection: "What happens if you adjust only one number?" Idea: "The answer changes by that much, so you have to fix it."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Changing both addends the same way in addition (Marco, Melissa). → "In adding, one goes up and one goes down."
- Changing subtraction numbers in opposite ways (452 − 197 → 449 − 200). → "In subtracting, both slide the same way — look at the number line."
- Adjusting only one number and forgetting to fix the answer (Yazmin).
- Adjusting into harder numbers. → Aim for a ten or hundred on the number you add/subtract.
- Fixing in the wrong direction (subtracting when they should add back).

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal:** Make addition and subtraction easier by changing the numbers in a way that keeps the answer the same.
- **Words:** adjust, friendly number, sum, difference, addend, equation.
- **Demonstrate:** (book) 224 + 109: move 1 from 224 to 109 → 223 + 110 = 333. 333 − 212: take 2 from both → 331 − 210 = 121. For addition use two piles of coins: move one coin from pile to pile — the total doesn't change. For subtraction draw a number line and slide both ends together — the gap doesn't change.
- **Ask:** "Which number is close to a ten or hundred?" · "Adding or subtracting — opposite or same?" · "Is the answer still the same?" · "If you only changed one number, how do you fix the answer?"
- **Checklist:** adjusts addition (opposite moves) · adjusts subtraction (same moves) · explains the difference between the two · shows same difference on a number line · picks valid adjustments · fixes a one-number adjustment · solves how-many-more stories.

## 13. New interaction or model needed

**M-ADJUST — adjust-arrows (new question type `adjust`):**
- Shows the original equation `a ○ b = ?` (○ = + or −). Under each number, an arrow down to an input box for the new number. As the child types, a small tag on each arrow shows the change (+3 / −3), computed from the inputs (no extra fields to fill). Then `new a ○ new b = [answer]`.
- Grading (any valid adjustment accepted):
  - Addition: new a + new b = a + b; **both** numbers changed by a non-zero amount (new a ≠ a and new b ≠ b); both new numbers ≥ 1; and the pair is not just the two numbers swapped (new a = b and new b = a). Answer = a + b.
  - Subtraction: new a − new b = a − b; both numbers changed by the same non-zero amount (new a ≠ a); both new numbers ≥ 1. Answer = a − b.
  - A swap, a +0 change, or a 0 ("443 + 0", "166 − 0") is **not an adjustment**: soft retry with "Change both numbers to make them easier — moving or swapping alone doesn't count." (not counted as a miss in Learn/Practice; marked wrong in the Test).
  - The "fix after changing one number" items (Step 4, PT8, PT11, O12, O13, T12) are the only place where a single changed number appears; they use `choice` + `num`, not M-ADJUST.
  - Feedback when the adjustment is invalid: addition — "You changed both numbers the same way. In adding, one goes up and the other goes down." subtraction — "You changed them opposite ways. In subtracting, change both the same way."
  - Optional soft note (not graded): if neither new number ends in 0, "Tip: try to make a ten or a hundred."
- `correctText`: one suggested adjustment (the one that makes b a multiple of 10) and the answer.
- Visual-only variant for slides (arrows with fixed tags).
- **Number line pair:** reuse `numberLineHTML` twice (stacked) with a `band` on each. In Learn slides the bands may carry a gap label ("255 apart"). **In question pictures (PT5, O8, T8) no picture may show the difference or any number the child types:** label only the two original endpoints (a and b); draw the adjusted band with unlabelled ticks at its ends, shifted by a fixed small amount (stated per item); no gap label. Each item shows exactly two candidate pictures: the correct one (adjusted band the same length as the original) and a wrong one whose adjusted band is visibly different — about 60 shorter (≥ 20% of the gap) — so the difference is obvious by eye. If `choice` cannot hold HTML, show the pictures above and label them A/B.
