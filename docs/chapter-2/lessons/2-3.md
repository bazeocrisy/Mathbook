## 2. Goals and prerequisites
Goals:
1. Know what an estimate is: a close answer, not the exact one.
2. Estimate a sum or difference by rounding each number to the nearest hundred.
3. Estimate by rounding each number to the nearest ten, and see that it is usually closer.
4. Estimate with compatible numbers (numbers ending in 00, 25, 50 or 75 that are easy to add or subtract in your head).
5. Choose the operation in a word problem and estimate (including a missing part and a two-step problem).
6. Use an estimate to decide whether an answer makes sense.

Prerequisites: Lesson 2-2 (round to the nearest 10 and 100); adding and subtracting multiples of 10 and 100 (for example 580 − 120, 400 + 300).

## 3. Vocabulary
- **Estimate**: a number close to the exact answer. "About how many?" asks for an estimate.
- **Exact answer**: the real answer you get from the real numbers.
- **Round**: change a number to the nearest ten or hundred.
- **Compatible numbers**: nearby numbers that are easy to work with in your head, like 575 and 125.
- **Sum**: the answer when you add. **Difference**: the answer when you subtract.
- **Reasonable**: makes sense; close to what you expected.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn
1. **Round each number, then add or subtract (nearest 100).** Book: 576 − 122 → 600 − 100 = 500. Rounding to hundreds gives numbers that are very easy to work with, but the estimate can be far off.
2. **Round each number to the nearest 10.** Book: 576 − 122 → 580 − 120 = 460. The numbers change less, so the estimate is closer to the exact answer (454).
3. **Compatible numbers.** Book: 576 − 122 → 575 − 125 = 450. Change each number to a nearby "friendly" number that is easy to compute with. In this app, for auto-graded items, compatible numbers are defined as **the nearest number ending in 00, 25, 50 or 75** (the nearest multiple of 25). For whole numbers this is always unique (no ties).
4. **Use an estimate to check an answer** (Choosing Tools box). If an exact answer is far from the estimate, check it again.

Expected-estimate rule for the whole lesson: **every app question names its method** ("round each number to the nearest hundred", "round each number to the nearest ten", or "use compatible numbers ending in 00, 25, 50 or 75"). With the method named, there is exactly one accepted estimate, and that is the only answer accepted. The book's open "reasonable estimate" wording is used only in the Parent Guide and in Practice Together explain items (parent review).

### Step 1 — What is an estimate? Round to the nearest hundred
- Explain: "An estimate is a close answer. Round each number to the nearest hundred. Then add."
- Example parts:
  1. "A book has 312 pages. Another has 465 pages. About how many pages in all?" Show the two numbers. Say: "'About' means we can estimate."
  2. Estimate arrows: 312 → 300, 465 → 500. "312 is closer to 300. 465 has a 6 in the tens place, so it rounds up to 500."
  3. "300 + 500 = 800. About 800 pages." Then: "The exact sum is 777. 800 is close to 777. That is a good estimate."
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `round100`. Prompt "Estimate a + b. Round each number to the nearest hundred." Parts: `num` "a rounds to", `num` "b rounds to", `num` "Estimate". Numbers: a, b in 101–899, not multiples of 100, and roundTo(a,100) + roundTo(b,100) ≤ 1,000; not demo numbers. Hint: "Look at the tens digit of each number. Then add the hundreds." Explanation: the two roundings (2-2 `digitWhy` wording) + the sum.

### Step 2 — Round to the nearest ten for a closer estimate
- Explain: "Rounding to the nearest ten changes the numbers less. The estimate is usually closer."
- Example parts:
  1. Same problem, 312 + 465. Arrows: 312 → 310, 465 → 470 (ones digit 5, round up).
  2. "310 + 470 = 780." Compare on one row: nearest hundred 800, nearest ten 780, exact 777. "780 is closer."
  3. "Hundreds are faster to add. Tens are closer. You choose." 
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `round10`. "Estimate a + b. Round each number to the nearest ten." Parts: two `num` roundings and `num` estimate. Numbers: a, b in 101–899, ones digit not 0, roundTo(a,10) + roundTo(b,10) ≤ 1,000; not demo numbers. Hint: "Look at the ones digit of each number."

### Step 3 — Estimate a difference
- Explain: "You can round to estimate when you subtract, too."
- Example parts:
  1. "A tree is 674 cm tall. A fence is 231 cm tall. About how much taller is the tree?" "How much taller" means subtract.
  2. Nearest ten: 674 → 670, 231 → 230. 670 − 230 = 440.
  3. Nearest hundred: 674 → 700, 231 → 200. 700 − 200 = 500.
  4. "The exact difference is 443. The nearest-ten estimate, 440, is very close."
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `diff`. Place chosen at random (10 or 100) and named in the prompt. "Estimate a − b. Round each number to the nearest [ten/hundred]." Parts: two roundings and the estimate. Numbers: 3-digit, a > b, rounded a > rounded b, not demo numbers. Sometimes show the "? = a − b" form (book items 2 and 6) so the child meets the unknown on the left.

### Step 4 — Compatible numbers
- Explain: "Compatible numbers are close numbers that are easy to work with. Numbers that end in 00, 25, 50 or 75 are easy to add and subtract."
- Example parts:
  1. 526 − 274. Arrows: 526 → 525, 274 → 275. "Think of quarters: 25, 50, 75, 100."
  2. 525 − 275 = 250. "The exact difference is 252."
  3. 247 + 352 → 250 + 350 = 600. "Exact: 599."
  4. "Compatible numbers are not always the same as rounding. 247 rounded to the nearest hundred is 200, but 250 is closer and still easy."
  5. "Working with 25s in your head: do the hundreds first." 750 − 325: 750 − 300 = 450, then 450 − 25 = 425. 375 + 450: 375 + 400 = 775, then 775 + 50 = 825. (These pairs are not used in any practice or test item.)
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `compat`. Prompt "Use compatible numbers (numbers ending in 00, 25, 50 or 75) to estimate a ± b." Generation: the **first** question on each visit needs no regrouping in the last two digits. For subtraction, (25k mod 100) ≥ (25m mod 100), e.g. 575 − 125. For addition, (25k mod 100) + (25m mod 100) ≤ 75, e.g. 250 + 325. A replacement question after two misses may need regrouping, e.g. 550 − 175. Never use the practice/test pairs (650/425, 225/350, 825/375, 475/250) or the slide pairs. Parts: `choice` "Which compatible numbers are closest?" (correct pair = nearest multiples of 25; distractors: the nearest-hundred pair, a pair with only one number changed, a pair changed the wrong way), and `num` "Estimate" (= result with the correct pair). Numbers: a = 25k ± 1 or ± 2, b = 25m ± 1 or ± 2, with at least one of 25k, 25m not a multiple of 100; for subtraction 25k > 25m; sum ≤ 1,000. Hint: "Find the nearest number that ends in 00, 25, 50 or 75."

### Step 5 — Estimate in word problems
- Explain: "Read the question. Decide: add or subtract? Then estimate."
- Example parts:
  1. "A zoo's bears eat 284 pounds of food one week and 517 pounds the next. About how much in the two weeks?" "In the two weeks" means put together: add. Nearest hundred: 300 + 500 = 800.
  2. "How many more are needed": "A sticker book holds 640 stickers. Jo has 389. About how many more does she need?" Subtract. Nearest ten: 640 − 390 = 250.
  3. "Still needs more, so how many now?" (book item 8 type): "A shelf holds 560 books. It still needs 213 more books to be full. About how many books are on it now?" The whole is 560 and 213 is the missing part, so subtract. Nearest ten: 560 − 210 = 350 (exact 347).
  4. Missing part from an estimate (book item 11 type): "Pia estimates she sold about 820 tickets on two nights. She sold 394 on the second night. About how many could she have sold on the first night?" Round 394 to the nearest ten: 390. 820 − 390 = 430.
  5. Two steps (book item 12 type): "A box has 455 beads. Lin uses 29 beads for each of 2 necklaces. About how many beads are left?" Round: 455 → 460, 29 → 30. Take away for each necklace: 460 − 30 = 430, 430 − 30 = 400. About 400 (exact 455 − 58 = 397).
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `word`. A generated story from these templates: "in all / altogether" → add; "how many more / how many left / how much taller" → subtract; "holds X, still needs Y more, how many now?" → subtract; "estimated total X, one part Y, about how many in the other part?" → subtract (round only Y; X is a multiple of 10); "has X, uses Y for each of 2" → subtract twice. Parts: `choice` "Add or subtract?" (Add / Subtract), then `num` "Estimate (round each number to the nearest [ten/hundred])". Hint: "Is the story putting things together, or finding what is left, what is missing, or how many more?"

### Step 6 — Check an answer with an estimate
- Explain: "An estimate helps you catch mistakes. If an answer is far from the estimate, check it again."
- Example parts:
  1. "Leo says 389 + 205 = 794." Estimate: 400 + 200 = 600. "794 is far from 600. Leo's answer is not reasonable." The exact sum is 594 (he added an extra hundred).
  2. "Ivy says 389 + 205 = 594." "594 is close to 600, so it is reasonable." "An estimate can't prove an answer is exactly right. It tells you if it is close."
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `check`. "Kim says a + b = c" (half the time) or "Kim says a − b = c" (the other half; a > b and roundTo(a,100) > roundTo(b,100)). "Estimate to check." Parts: `num` "Estimate (nearest hundred)", `choice` "Is Kim's answer reasonable?" (✓ "Yes. It is close to the estimate." / "No. It is far from the estimate."). Generation: half the time c = exact; otherwise c = exact ± 200 or ± 300 (never within 150 of the estimate), c > 0. The choices never print the estimate.
- Reflection (Learn completion screen, ungraded): "When might you estimate a sum or difference in your life?" Idea: "Shopping (about how much will it cost?), trips (about how far?), saving (about how much more do I need?)."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching
- **Rounding the answer instead of the numbers** (computing 278 + 416 = 694, then rounding). Say: "Estimate first, so you don't have to do the hard math."
- **Rounding only one number.** Ask: "Did you round both numbers?"
- **Wrong digit or halfway rounded down** (from 2-2). Ask: "Which digit do you look at?"
- **Adding when the story needs subtraction** ("needs 288 more" is not "add"). Ask: "Is the whole known? Are we finding a part?"
- **Thinking an estimate must equal the exact answer**, or that a close answer must be right. Say: "An estimate tells you if an answer is close, not if it is exactly right."
- **Compatible numbers that are far away** (651 → 700). Ask: "Is there a friendly number closer than that?"
