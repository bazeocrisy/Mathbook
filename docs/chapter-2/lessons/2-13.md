## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goal: *I can compare two numbers up to 9,999 using a number line or place value, and write <, >, =, or ≠.*

- Plot/locate numbers on a number line; the number farther right is greater.
- Compare digits from the greatest place (left) to the least place; the first place that is different decides.
- Use the symbols <, >, = and ≠ correctly and read them aloud.
- Compare a 4-digit number with a 3-digit number (the 3-digit number has 0 thousands).
- Order three numbers; answer a real-world "which is more" question.

Prerequisites: 2-1 (place value to 9,999: digits and their values), reading number lines (2-2).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Compare** — decide which number is greater, which is less, or if they are equal.
- **Greater than ( > )** — is more than. 5,200 > 4,900.
- **Less than ( < )** — is fewer than. 4,900 < 5,200.
- **Equal to ( = )** — is the same amount as.
- **Not equal to ( ≠ )** — is not the same amount as.
- **Place value** — the value of a digit because of its place (thousands, hundreds, tens, ones).
- Tip for the child: the open side of < or > faces the greater number ("the mouth eats the bigger number").

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

1. **Number line** (book "One Way"). Book example: 1,249 and 1,324 plotted on 1,000–1,500. Numbers grow to the right, so the number farther right is greater. Reasoning: a number line is in order; position shows size.
2. **Place value** (book "Another Way"). Line the numbers up by place. Compare the thousands first. If they are the same, compare the hundreds, then the tens, then the ones. The first place where the digits differ decides. Book example: same thousands (1), hundreds 3 vs 2, so 1,324 > 1,249. Reasoning: one more in a bigger place is worth more than anything in the smaller places (1 hundred is more than 99).
3. **= and ≠**: two numbers are equal only if every place has the same digit. If any place is different, they are not equal (≠) — and then one is < and the other >.
4. Implicit on the On My Own page: **different numbers of digits** (4,349 vs 349) — a 3-digit number has 0 thousands, so any 4-digit number is greater; and **ordering three numbers** (Extend).

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Five steps. Each Example is a short slide series; Your Turn is one generated check (two misses → explain and new question, as in 2-2). Learn demo numbers (never reused in Your Turn): 2,371; 2,436; 5,362; 5,318; 6,207; 6,270; 3,158; 958; 4,572; 3,986; 4,519.

### Step 1 · Compare on a number line
Example slides (F5 number line with `points`):
1. "Nia has 2,436 stickers. Omar has 2,371. Who has more?" Show line 2,000–2,500, major ticks every 100, labels at each hundred.
2. Plot 2,371 (dot + label). "2,371 is between 2,300 and 2,400."
3. Plot 2,436. "2,436 is between 2,400 and 2,500."
4. "Numbers get bigger as you go right. 2,436 is farther right." Show **2,436 > 2,371** and **2,371 < 2,436**. "Nia has more."

Your Turn (`parts`):
- Generate a hundreds-wide window: L = random multiple of 500 from 1,000 to 8,500; line L to L+500 with major ticks every 100. Pick two different numbers a, b in (L, L+500), not multiples of 10, at least 40 apart so the dots do not overlap. Show both dots, labeled **A** and **B** only (left/right order random).
- Part 1 `choice`: "Which dot shows {a}?" choices A / B.
- Part 2 `symbol` (A1): "{a} ◯ {b}" with choices <, >, =.
- Hint: "Find the hundred each number is in. The number farther right is greater."
- Explanation: "{a} is between X and Y; {b} is between …; {greater} is farther right, so {a} {sym} {b}."

### Step 2 · Compare with place value
Example slides (F10 compare chart: the column being compared is highlighted):
1. "Compare 5,362 and 5,318. Line them up by place."
2. Thousands: 5 and 5 — same. Move right.
3. Hundreds: 3 and 3 — same. Move right.
4. Tens: 6 and 1 — different! 6 tens > 1 ten, so **5,362 > 5,318**. "We can stop. The ones do not matter now."
5. Book-style check: "Why can we stop? 6 tens is 60. Even 9 ones can't make up 50 more."

Your Turn (`parts`):
- Generate a (4-digit) and b by copying a and changing one digit at a random place P (thousands, hundreds, tens or ones), and optionally changing a digit to the right of P too (so the child must ignore it). Never equal. Keep a, b in 1,000–9,999.
- Part 1 `choice`: "Which place decides?" Thousands / Hundreds / Tens / Ones.
- Part 2 `symbol`: <, >, =.
- Hint: "Start at the left. Find the first place where the digits are different."

### Step 3 · Equal or not equal
Example slides:
1. "6,207 and 6,207: thousands same, hundreds same, tens same, ones same. **6,207 = 6,207**."
2. "6,207 and 6,270: the tens are different (0 and 7). So **6,207 ≠ 6,270**." Show the ≠ sign: "= with a slash means *not equal*."
3. "If they are not equal, one is greater: 6,270 > 6,207."

Your Turn (`parts`):
- 50%: identical pair; 50%: pair made by swapping two neighbouring digits of a (e.g. 4,815 vs 4,851) — the "looks the same" trap. The two swapped digits must be **different**, so the pair is never identical.
- Part 1 `symbol` with choices = / ≠.
- Part 2 `choice` (`compact`): "Which place is different first?" — Thousands / Hundreds / Tens / Ones / "No place — every digit is the same". Key: the **first** (leftmost) differing place, or "No place" for an identical pair. In a swap pair two places differ; the question asks for the *first*, so the second differing place is a wrong answer, not a second true one.
- Hint: "Check every place, from left to right."

### Step 4 · Different numbers of digits
Example slides:
1. "Compare 3,158 and 958. Ivy says 958 is greater because 9 > 3. Let's check."
2. Put both in the chart: 958 has **no thousands** — write a 0 in the thousands column (0,958 shown faintly).
3. "Thousands: 3 and 0. 3 > 0, so **3,158 > 958**. Ivy compared the wrong places."
4. "Any 4-digit number is greater than any 3-digit number." Small number line 0–4,000 with 958 and 3,158 plotted to confirm.

Your Turn (`parts`):
- a = random 4-digit with thousands digit **1–8**; b = random 3-digit whose first digit is greater than a's first digit (so the trap is live). Random order left/right.
- Part 1 `symbol` (<, >, =).
- Part 2 `choice`: "Max says {b} is greater because {b's first digit} > {a's first digit}. Is Max right?" → key "No. {b} has 0 thousands. Compare the thousands first." (names where to look, never "less", so it does not give away Part 1) / "Yes. Compare the first digits." / "Yes. {b's first digit} is bigger than {a's first digit}."
- Hint: "How many thousands does each number have?"

### Step 5 · Order numbers and solve problems
Example slides:
1. "Three bike trails: 4,572 m, 3,986 m, 4,519 m. Order from least to greatest."
2. Thousands: 3,986 has 3 thousands — it is the least.
3. 4,572 and 4,519 both have 4 thousands and 5 hundreds; tens 7 vs 1 → 4,519 < 4,572.
4. "**3,986 < 4,519 < 4,572**." Show on a number line 3,500–5,000 with three dots.
5. Real world: "Which trail is longest? The greatest number: 4,572 m."

Your Turn (`parts`):
- Three different 4-digit numbers: two share the thousands digit and hundreds digit (decided at tens), one has a smaller or larger thousands digit.
- Part 1 `select` (drop-down in a sentence): "___ < ___ < ___" each drop-down offers the three numbers (graded as the exact order). (Alternatively three `choice` parts labeled Least / Middle / Greatest.)
- Part 2 `choice`: "Which is greatest?" (one of the three as a word-problem label, e.g. "Which trail is longest?").
- Hint: "Find the least first by looking at the thousands. Then compare the two that are left."

Learn reflection (`seeIt.reflection`, never graded): "What did you notice about comparing 4-digit numbers?" Idea: "Start with the thousands. The first place that is different tells you which is greater. A number line shows the same thing — the greater number is farther right."

## 6. Practice Together (guided)

Item formats: `cmp` = `parts` with a `symbol` part (§13); "dot" = `choice` "Which dot shows …?" on a number line with dots A and B.

**Reason choices (written out once, used by every item that asks "why"):**
- For < / > items — `choice` (`compact`) "Which place decides?": **Thousands / Hundreds / Tens / Ones**. Key = the first place where the digits differ. The choices name a place, never "greater" or "less", so they never give away the `symbol` part.
- For = / ≠ items — `choice` (`compact`) "Which place is different first?": **Thousands / Hundreds / Tens / Ones / No place — every digit is the same**. Key = the first differing place, or "No place".

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Comparing the first digits of numbers with different lengths (958 vs 3,158). Fix: write a 0 placeholder so places line up.
- Stopping too early or too late: deciding by the ones when the hundreds differ. Fix: always go left to right; stop at the first difference.
- Thinking same digits mean equal (2,814 vs 2,841). Fix: compare place by place.
- Reversing < and >. Fix: read the sentence aloud ("2,814 **is less than** 2,841"); the open side faces the greater number.
- Number lines: reading a "by 50s" line as "by 100s". Fix: find the step between two labels first.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal**: compare numbers to 9,999 with a number line and with place value; use <, >, =, ≠.
- **Words**: compare, greater than (>), less than (<), equal to (=), not equal to (≠), place value.
- **Demonstrate**: (1) Draw a line 2,000–2,500; plot 2,371 and 2,436; "farther right is greater." (2) Write 5,362 over 5,318; cover all but the thousands; uncover one place at a time until the digits differ. (3) Write 3,158 over 958 and fill in a 0 in front of 958.
- **Ask**: "Which place do you look at first?" "Where are they first different?" "How do you know you can stop?" "Read your sentence aloud — does it make sense?" "Which is farther right on the number line?"
- **Mistakes**: as §10.
- **Checklist**: locates two numbers on a number line and says which is greater · compares by place value from the left · uses = and ≠ correctly · handles a 3-digit vs 4-digit pair · orders three numbers · answers a "which is more" word problem.
