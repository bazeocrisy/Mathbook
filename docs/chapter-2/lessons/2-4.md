## 2. Goals and prerequisites
Goals:
1. Know that changing the **order** of addends does not change the sum (commutative property), and use it to fill in an equation without adding.
2. Know that changing the **grouping** (which two you add first) does not change the sum (associative property).
3. Look for a **friendly pair**: two addends that make a number ending in 0 or 00, and add them first.
4. Recognise every expression that gives the same total (select all), and reject ones with subtraction or changed numbers.
5. Explain why a way is more efficient.

Prerequisites: adding multiples of 10 and 100 to a 3-digit number; make-a-ten facts (1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6, 5 + 5); 2-digit addition.

Note on sums: every final step in this lesson adds a multiple of 100 (or a number ending in 0) to another number **with no regrouping**, so a child who has not yet learned 3-digit regrouping (2-6, 2-10) can do it in their head.

## 3. Vocabulary
- **Addend**: a number you add. In 34 + 66, the addends are 34 and 66.
- **Sum**: the answer when you add.
- **Equation**: a number sentence with an equal sign. Both sides have the same value.
- **Order property** (grown-up name: Commutative Property of Addition): you can switch the order of addends, and the sum stays the same.
- **Grouping property** (grown-up name: Associative Property of Addition): you can choose which addends to add first, and the sum stays the same.
- **Friendly pair**: two addends whose sum ends in 0 or 00, like 46 and 54 (100) or 135 and 215 (350).
- **Efficient**: a faster, easier way that still gives the right answer.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn
1. **Group addends any way (associative).** Book: 27 + 53 + 40. Add 27 + 53 = 80 first, then 80 + 40 = 120. Or add 53 + 40 = 93 first, then 27 + 93 = 120. The sum is the same. Grouping 27 and 53 first is easier because 7 + 3 makes a ten. The book draws a V joining the two addends added first.
2. **Switch the order (commutative).** Book: 27 + 53 + 40 = 120 and 40 + 27 + 53 = 120. The sum does not change.
3. **Use the properties to work efficiently.** Book Work Together: 797 + 111 = ___ + 797. You do not need to add. The same two addends are on both sides, so the blank is 111. Book items 7–12 combine the two: find a friendly pair, add it first, then add the third number.

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Visual used in steps 2–5 (see section 13): the **grouping V**, the book's picture of two addends joined by a V to the sum of that pair, with the remaining addend carried down:

```
 34 + 50 + 66
  \_______/
    100   + 50 = 150
```
(drawn with the V from the 1st and 3rd addends when the friendly pair is not next to each other).

Demo numbers (Your Turn never repeats them): 36, 18, 245, 132, 34, 50, 66, 146, 289, 54, 125, 431, 215, 29, 45, 71, 643, 258, 340, 185.

### Step 1 — Switch the order
- Explain: "You can add numbers in any order. The sum stays the same."
- Example parts:
  1. Two rows of counters: 4 red then 3 blue, and 3 blue then 4 red. "4 + 3 = 7. 3 + 4 = 7. Same counters, same sum."
  2. "36 + 18 = 54 and 18 + 36 = 54."
  3. "This works with big numbers too: 245 + 132 = 132 + 245." Both are 377. "So in 245 + 132 = 132 + ___, the blank is 245. You don't even need to add!"
- Your Turn: `number`, skill `order`. One of four forms (book items 1–4): `a + b = b + __`, `a + __ = b + a`, `a + b = __ + a`, `__ + b = b + a`. Answer = the missing addend. Numbers: a in 101–899, b in 10–899, a ≠ b, digits of a and b not reverses of each other. Hint: "Find the number that is on one side but missing on the other side." Explanation: "Both sides add the same two numbers, a and b. The order changed, so the sum is the same. The missing number is __."

### Step 2 — Group the addends
- Explain: "With three addends, you can choose which two to add first. The sum stays the same."
- Example parts:
  1. "Mia found 34 shells on Monday, 50 on Tuesday and 66 on Wednesday. How many shells?"
  2. Way 1 (V on 34 and 50): 34 + 50 = 84, then 84 + 66 = 150.
  3. Way 2 (V on 34 and 66): 34 + 66 = 100, then 100 + 50 = 150. "Same sum! 4 + 6 makes a ten, and 3 tens + 6 tens + 1 ten make 100."
  4. "Which way was easier? Adding to 100 is easy."
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `group`. "Add the three addends. Add x and y first." Parts: `num` "x + y =", `num` "Total:". Generation, so the first answer is not always the same:
  - Pick the pair total P from {60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 200, 300}.
  - Pick x with ones digit not 0, and set y = P − x. Both are at least 11; when P ≥ 200, one of them is 3-digit.
  - Third addend z: if P is a multiple of 100, z is a multiple of 10 from 20 to 90. Otherwise z is a multiple of 100 from 100 to 600. Either way P + z needs no regrouping.
  - Put the pair in a random two of the three positions (1st and 2nd, 1st and 3rd, or 2nd and 3rd). The prompt names the pair.
  - Avoid demo numbers.
  - Hint: "Which ones digits make 10?"
  - Examples: 37 + 40 + 63 → 100, 140; 300 + 26 + 44 → 70, 370; 154 + 46 + 50 → 200, 250.

### Step 3 — Find a friendly pair
- Explain: "Look for two addends that make a number ending in 00, or at least ending in 0. Add them first."
- Example parts:
  1. 146 + 289 + 54. "Look at the ones digits: 6 and 4 make 10. Try 146 + 54."
  2. V on 146 and 54: 146 + 54 = 200 (6 + 4 = 10 ones → 1 ten; 4 tens + 5 tens + 1 ten = 10 tens → 1 hundred; 1 hundred + 1 hundred = 2 hundreds).
  3. 200 + 289 = 489.
  4. "A pair that ends in 0 helps too." 125 + 431 + 215: 5 + 5 make 10, so V on 125 and 215: 125 + 215 = 340. Then 340 + 431 = 771. (Other pairs: 125 + 431 = 556 and 431 + 215 = 646, which do not end in 0.)
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `friendly`. Parts: `choice` "Which two addends make a number ending in 00?" (or "… ending in 0?" for the ends-in-0 kind), listing the three pairs as "x + y"; `num` "Their sum:"; `num` "Total:". Generation:
  - Usually (about two questions in three): H is a multiple of 100 from 200 to 700. x is in 101–(H − 11) with ones digit not 0, and y = H − x. z is a 2- or 3-digit number with H + z ≤ 999, and z does not make 00 with x or y. The other two pairs must not end in 00.
  - Otherwise, the ends-in-0 kind: H is a multiple of 10 but not of 100, with H ≤ 600. x + y = H, and exactly one pair ends in 0 (the other two do not end in 0). z is chosen so that H + z needs no regrouping: (tens of H) + (tens of z) ≤ 9, (ones of z) any digit, and H + z ≤ 999.
  - Shuffle the order of x, y and z.
  - Hint: "Look at the ones digits first. Which two make 10? Then check the tens."

### Step 4 — Same addends, same total
- Explain: "Any order of the same addends gives the same total. Changing a number or subtracting changes the total."
- Example parts:
  1. Table "Seeds Planted": Monday 29, Tuesday 45, Wednesday 71.
  2. "29 + 45 + 71 ✓, 71 + 29 + 45 ✓, 45 + 71 + 29 ✓ — all the same addends."
  3. "71 − 45 + 29 ✗ — that subtracts. 29 + 54 + 71 ✗ — 54 is not 45; the digits are switched."
  4. Total: 29 + 71 = 100, 100 + 45 = 145.
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `same`. Table with three 2-digit values p, q, r where p + r = 100. Parts: `multi` "Which show the total? Choose all." Choices (shuffled): two reorderings of p, q, r (✓), the original order (✓), one with subtraction (✗), one with q's digits reversed (✗; q has two different non-zero digits), one with a value changed by 10 (✗). `num` "Total:" (100 + q). Hint: "Check that each one adds the same three numbers."

### Step 5 — Add more efficiently
- Explain: "Use the properties to save work."
- Example parts:
  1. "Kayla must fill in 643 + 258 = ___ + 643. She adds 643 + 258, then subtracts 643. That works, but it is slow."
  2. "Faster: both sides must have the same addends. 643 is on both sides, so the blank is 258. No adding needed."
  3. "A receipt shows $215, $340 and $185. Group $215 + $185 = $400 first (5 + 5 = 10). Then $400 + $340 = $740."
- Your Turn: `parts`, skill `efficient`. Alternates between: (a) "Fill in a + b = __ + a. What is the fastest way?" `choice` ✓ "Switch the order. Both sides must have the same two addends, so the blank is the addend missing from that side." / "Add the two numbers, then subtract the first number." / "Add the two numbers, then add the first number again." / "Subtract the smaller number from the bigger number." (no choice names a value) and `num` blank = b; (b) a three-price receipt: `choice` "Which prices should you add first?" (the three pairs, without their sums) and `num` total. Receipt constraints are the same as step 3's usual kind: H, the sum of the pair, is a multiple of 100 from 200 to 700; exactly one pair ends in 00 and the other two pairs do not; z is the third price with H + z ≤ 999 (no regrouping, because H ends in 00).
- Reflection (Learn completion screen, ungraded): "How can changing the order of three addends help you add faster?" Idea: "You can put two addends that make a ten or a hundred next to each other and add them first."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching
- **Doing extra work** (adding then subtracting to fill a blank). Ask: "What is on both sides already?"
- **Copying the wrong number into the blank** (writing the number that is already on that side). Ask: "Which number is missing from that side?"
- **Missing switched digits** (42 vs 24, 381 vs 318). Say: "Read every digit."
- **Thinking subtraction can be moved around like addition** (76 − 53 + 47). Say: "Only adding can be switched around freely."
- **Adding left to right only** and missing the friendly pair. Ask: "Which ones digits make 10?"
- **Ones make 10 but forgetting the extra ten** (263 + 137 = 390). Say: "10 ones make 1 more ten."

## 11. Parent Guide content
- Goal: "Switch the order or change the grouping to add more easily. The sum does not change."
- Words: addend, sum, equation, order property, grouping property, friendly pair, efficient.
- Demonstrate:
  1. Show 4 + 3 and 3 + 4 with objects. Same sum.
  2. Write 245 + 132 = 132 + ___. Ask: "Do we need to add?" (No: 245.)
  3. Write 34 + 50 + 66. Add left to right (84, 150), then add 34 + 66 first (100, 150). Same sum; the second way is easier.
  4. Write 146 + 289 + 54. Circle 6 and 4. Add 146 + 54 = 200, then 489.
  5. Book example: Billy's tickets 27 + 53 + 40 = 120 (27 + 53 = 80 first).
- Ask: "Which two addends make a ten or a hundred?"; "Does the order change the sum?"; "Is there a faster way?"; "Is every number the same on both sides?"
- Mistakes: section 10.
- Checklist:
  - Fills in a missing addend without adding (all four blank positions).
  - Finds the friendly pair, adds it first, and finds the total.
  - Chooses every expression with the same addends; rejects subtraction and switched digits.
  - Explains why a way is more efficient.
  - Scores 90% or higher on the Addition Properties Test.
