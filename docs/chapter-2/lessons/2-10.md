## 2. Goals and prerequisites

Goals
1. Add two 3-digit numbers (sum ≤ 999) with **partial sums** — written in a row and in a column.
2. Add by **adjusting addends** (take from one, give to the other) to make a ten or hundred.
3. Judge whether a strategy is valid (both methods work; "add 3 then subtract 3 from the sum" works).
4. Solve with the sum on either side (`___ = 123 + 443`).
5. Word problems: pick the useful information, add the same number twice, find how many packs are enough, two additions in a row.

Prerequisites: 2-4 to 2-6 (properties, patterns, partial sums), 2-8 (adjust numbers).

## 3. Vocabulary

- **Addend** — a number being added.
- **Sum** — the answer to an addition problem.
- **Partial sums** — add the hundreds, the tens, and the ones separately, then add those answers.
- **Adjust addends** — take some from one addend and give it to the other so the numbers are easier. The sum stays the same.
- **Strategy** — a way to solve a problem.
- **Efficient** — quick and easy for you, with few steps.
- **Extra information** — a number in a story that you do not need.

## 4. Methods taught in Learn

1. **Partial sums (row).** Decompose each addend by place: `700 + 100 = 800`, `20 + 60 = 80`, `2 + 9 = 11`; add the partials `800 + 80 + 11 = 891`. Why: every hundred, ten and one gets counted once.
2. **Partial sums (column).** Same partials written under the problem in a column, then added.
3. **Adjust addends.** `722 + 169`: take 1 from 722 and give it to 169 → `721 + 170 = 891`. The total does not change because nothing was added or lost overall.
4. **Choose a strategy.** Any valid strategy is fine. Adjusting is quick when an addend is close to a ten or hundred (169, 299); partial sums always work.
5. **Useful information** (Exploring box): 350 in the Learn story is not needed for the total now.

## 5. Guided sequence (Learn steps)

Five steps. Examples use original numbers 634, 259, 893, 356, 218, 574, 336, 457, 410 (book example 722 + 169 = 891 may be cited in the Parent Guide). Your Turn never repeats them.

### Step 1 — Partial sums in a row
Example slides:
1. "`634 + 259`. Break each number into hundreds, tens, and ones."
2. `600 + 200 = 800` (hundreds highlighted).
3. `30 + 50 = 80` (tens), `4 + 9 = 13` (ones).
4. `800 + 80 + 13 = 893`. "Add the partial sums."

Your Turn (`parts`): "Add `a + b` with partial sums." Parts: `num` "Hundreds:" `num` "Tens:" `num` "Ones:" `num` "Sum:". Generation: a, b 3-digit, a + b ≤ 999, no digit 0 in tens/ones of a or b, ones sum ≥ 10 (so the ones partial is a 2-digit number like 13). Graded: all four.
Hint: "Add the hundreds first. Then the tens. Then the ones. Then add those three answers."

### Step 2 — Partial sums in a column
Example slides (vertical stack, see §13):
1. 634 over +259 with a line.
2. Partials appear one per row: 800, 80, 13 (with "600 + 200", "30 + 50", "4 + 9" written beside each).
3. Line, total **893**. "Same numbers, written in a column."

Your Turn (`parts`): same four fields, displayed with the column layout (problem only, partial rows blank). Generation: tens sum ≥ 100 this time (e.g. tens digits 6 and 7 → 130), sum ≤ 999.
Hint: "The tens can make more than 100. 60 + 70 = 130."

### Step 3 — Adjust addends
Example slides:
1. `634 + 259`. "259 is 1 away from 260."
2. Adjust arrows: −1 under 634, +1 under 259 → `633 + 260`.
3. `633 + 260 = 893`. "Take 1 from one addend and give it to the other. The sum stays the same."
4. Second example: `356 + 218` → −2 / +2 → `354 + 220 = 574`.

Your Turn (`parts`): "Make `b` a whole ten (or hundred). Then add." Parts: `num` "b becomes:" (b rounded up to the next ten — or hundred when b ends in 9x), `num` "a becomes:" (a − k), `num` "Sum:". Generation: b ones digit 7, 8 or 9 (k = 10 − ones) or b = x98/x99 (k to next hundred); a ones digit ≥ k so a − k has no borrowing; sum ≤ 999. Grade: all three (the instruction fixes which addend changes).
Hint: "How much does b need to reach the next ten? Take that much from a."

### Step 4 — Does this strategy work?
Example slides:
1. Work Together type: "Ana adjusts `356 + 218` and gets 574. Dan uses partial sums: 500 + 60 + 14 = 574. Can either strategy be used?" → "Yes. Both are correct ways; they give the same sum."
2. Another way to adjust: "Priya does `336 + 457`: she adds 4 to 336 → `340 + 457 = 797`. Then she takes 4 away from the sum: 797 − 4 = **793**." "She added 4 extra, so she takes 4 back. It works!"
3. Warning: "If she forgot to take the 4 back, 797 would be 4 too many."

Your Turn (`parts`): generated: "Kim adds `a + b`. She adds k to a to get (a + k) + b = S. Then she ___ k." Variant chosen at random: "subtracts k from the sum" (valid) or "adds k to the sum" (invalid). `choice` "Do you agree?" [✓ correct statement for the variant, e.g. "Yes. She added k too many, so she takes k away." / "No. She added k too many, so she must take k away, not add it."; plus 2 distractors] and `num` "The correct sum:" a + b. Generation: a ones digit 7–9, k = 10 − ones, sum ≤ 999.
Hint: "Did she add extra? Then what must she do to the sum?"

### Step 5 — Choose a strategy and use the useful numbers
Example slides:
1. Story: "The library grew from **410** to **634** books last year. This year **259** more came. How many books now?" "Which numbers do we need? **634** and **259**. 410 was last year's start — we don't need it."
2. "634 + 259 = 893 books." "Pick a strategy you like. Adjusting is quick when a number is close to a ten or hundred. Partial sums always work."
3. Two additions in a row (book #12 structure, original numbers): "A swim club had **121** swimmers in fall and **246** in winter. Summer had **110** more than fall and winter together." `121 + 246 = 367`, then `367 + 110 = 477`.

Your Turn (`parts`): story with three numbers, one not needed (e.g., "A school raised $a at a fair and $b at a bake sale. They spent $c on books. How much did they raise?"). `choice` "Which number do you NOT need?" [$c ✓, $a, $b]; `num` "Raised: $" a + b. Generation: a, b 3-digit, a + b ≤ 999, c a multiple of 50 less than a + b.
Hint: "What does the question ask? Find the numbers that answer it."

`seeIt.reflection`: "How do you decide which addition strategy to use?" Idea: "Look at the numbers. If one is close to a ten or hundred, adjust. Otherwise, partial sums always work. Use the one that is quick and makes sense to you."

## 10. Common mistakes and coaching

- Writing the ones partial `11` as `1` and losing the ten — "11 is one ten and one one; keep both."
- Adjusting the same direction on both addends (`635 + 260`) — "Take from one, give to the other."
- Forgetting to undo a change made to only one addend (Priya/Ty type).
- Using every number in a story — "What does the question ask?"
- Packs: stopping below the target (2 packs = 500 < 700) — "Is that enough?"
- Tens partial over 100 (60 + 80 = 140) written as 14 — read it as tens.

## 11. Parent Guide content

- **Goal:** Add 3-digit numbers fluently with partial sums or adjusting, and choose a strategy.
- **Words:** addend, sum, partial sums, adjust addends, strategy, efficient, extra information.
- **Demonstrate:** Write `634 + 259`; do 800, 80, 13 → 893 in a row, then in a column. Then show 633 + 260 = 893 with −1/+1 arrows. (Book example: 722 + 169 = 891.)
- **Ask:** "Is one number close to a ten or hundred?" "Did your sum stay the same when you adjusted?" "Which numbers does the question need?" "Is that enough?"
- **Checklist:** partial sums in a row and column; adjusts correctly; decides whether a strategy is valid; solves sum-on-the-left; ignores extra information; solves packs and two-step problems.
