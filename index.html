Chapter 2 • Lesson 2-1

Represent 4-Digit Numbers

Place value • standard form • expanded form • word form

Parent: Teach It

Goal: Chris identifies the value of each digit in a four-digit number and writes the number in standard, expanded, and word form.

What to say

“A digit has a place. Its place tells us how much it is worth. Every place to the left is ten times the value of the place before it.”

  1. Write 2,138. Read it aloud: “Two thousand, one hundred thirty-eight.”
  2. Point from right to left: 8 ones, 3 tens, 1 hundred, 2 thousands.
  3. Say: “3 tens means 30, not 3. And 2 thousands means 2,000.”
  4. Expand it: 2,000 + 100 + 30 + 8 = 2,138.
  5. Ask Chris to explain each digit without your help.
Ask: Why is the 3 worth 30? Listen for: “It is in the tens place.”
Ask: If the 1 changed to 5, what would the new number be? Answer: 2,538.

Next: Open “See It” and use the chart together. Then complete the textbook problems on pages 35–36.

See It: Build a Number

Change the number to show how each digit changes value. This is a place-value model, not a picture of actual base-ten blocks.

Visual groups (up to nine per place)

Teach: 10 ones trade for 1 ten. 10 tens trade for 1 hundred. 10 hundreds trade for 1 thousand.

Ask Chris: What happens to the value when a digit moves one place left?

Practice Together

Help is allowed. Read each question, let Chris answer, and discuss the explanation.

Practice complete. Chris can now try the independent test.

Independent Lesson Test

10 questions. No hints until submitting. Chris may use scratch paper. Parent can read directions aloud without explaining the answers.

Note: All ten are automatically scored. Discuss his reasoning afterward, too.
